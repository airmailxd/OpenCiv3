#GAME_CONCEPTS

#GCON_Age_of_Science
Age of Science
^An [Age of Science] is a period of 20 turns during which every city of a civilization produces 25% more
$LINK<research=GCON_Research>. It can only be started by a scientific $LINK<leader=GCON_Leaders>, who is used
up in the process.

#GCON_Agreements
Diplomatic Agreements
^Once you have an $LINK<embassy=GCON_Embassies> with another civilization, you can make [agreements] with it
at the negotiating table. Most agreements last for a set number of turns, after which they can be renewed.
^
^{Right of Passage}
^Each side's units may move through the other's $LINK<territory=GCON_Territory> and use its roads and railroads.
Needs $LINK<Map Making=TECH_Map_Making>.
^
^{Military Alliance}
^Both sides agree to fight a common enemy.
^
^{Mutual Protection Pact}
^Each side promises to declare war on anyone who attacks the other.
^
^{Trade Embargo}
^Both sides agree not to $LINK<trade=GCON_Trade> with a third civilization.
^
^Advances, gold, $LINK<maps=GCON_Maps>, $LINK<resources=GCON_ResourcesN>, cities and contact with other
civilizations can also be exchanged. Breaking an agreement by attacking a partner damages your reputation with
every civilization that hears of it.

#GCON_Air_Missions
Air Missions
^Aircraft do not walk across the map like other units. They are based in a city, on an
$LINK<airfield=GCON_Airfields> or on a $LINK<carrier=PRTO_Carrier>, and fly [missions] to targets within their
operational range, returning to base afterwards.
^
^{Bombing}
^The aircraft attacks a target square, damaging units, and in cities possibly buildings or population.
^
^{Air Superiority}
^The aircraft patrols the area around its base and tries to intercept enemy aircraft that fly into it.
^
^{Recon}
^The aircraft flies over a distant area and reveals what is there.
^
^{Re-base}
^The aircraft moves to another city, airfield or carrier further away than its normal range.
^
^{Precision Strike}
^Stealth aircraft can aim at a single building in a city.
^
^Enemy fighters on air superiority, $LINK<SAM Missile Batteries=BLDG_SAM_Missile_Battery> and anti-aircraft
units can shoot down aircraft on their way to a target.

#GCON_Airfields
Airfields
^An [airfield] is a landing strip that a $LINK<worker=PRTO_Worker> can build out in the countryside once
$LINK<Flight=TECH_Flight> is known. Aircraft can be based there and fly missions from it just as from a city
with an $LINK<airport=BLDG_Airport>. Building an airfield uses up the worker. An airfield belongs to its builder,
but if it ends up inside another civilization's borders it passes to that civilization, and it is destroyed if
pillaged.

#GCON_Armies
Armies
^An [army] is a single unit that carries other land units inside it, which move together and fight as one.
When an army is attacked or attacks, the units inside take turns fighting, so an army can survive a battle that
would have destroyed any one of them.
^
^{Forming an Army}
^A military $LINK<leader=GCON_Leaders> can be turned into an empty $LINK<army=PRTO_Army>, and a city with the
$LINK<Military Academy=BLDG_Military_Academy> can build armies. Units are then loaded into it. An army holds three
units, or four once you own $LINK<The Pentagon=BLDG_Pentagon>.
^
^{Limits}
^A civilization can only support one army for every four cities it has.

#GCON_Assimilation
Assimilation
^[Assimilation] is how $LINK<foreign nationals=GCON_Nationality> in your cities slowly come to think of
themselves as your own people. The longer they live under your rule in peace, the more of them change their
nationality. Assimilated citizens no longer side with their old homeland during a war or fall for its
$LINK<propaganda=GCON_Espionage>.

#GCON_Barbarians
Barbarians
^[Barbarians] are tribes that belong to no civilization. They live in camps scattered in the wilderness and send
raiding parties of warriors and horsemen against nearby cities, and in some places arrive by sea in galleys.
How often they appear depends on the barbarian level chosen when the game is set up.
^
^Destroying a barbarian camp ends its raids and is usually rewarded with gold. $LINK<The Great Wall=BLDG_Great_Wall> doubles your
units' strength against barbarians. Barbarians that reach an undefended city can sack it.
^
^Small villages left by vanished peoples also dot the land. Entering one may bring gold, an advance, a new unit,
or a band of hostile warriors.

#GCON_Barricades
Barricades
^A [barricade] is a strengthened $LINK<fortress=GCON_Fortresses>. Once $LINK<Construction=TECH_Construction>
is known, a $LINK<worker=PRTO_Worker> can turn a fortress into a barricade. Units defending in a barricade get
+100% defense instead of the fortress's +50%, and enemy land units entering the square must stop there even if
it is empty. A pillaged barricade falls back to a fortress.

#GCON_Bombardment_in_Ports
Bombardment in Ports
^Ships tied up in a harbor cannot steer clear of incoming fire. When a coastal city is
$LINK<bombarded=GCON_Combat>, the ships in its port are especially likely to be hit.

#GCON_Colony
Colony
^A [colony] is a small settlement a $LINK<worker=PRTO_Worker> builds on a tradable
$LINK<resource=GCON_ResourcesN> that lies outside your $LINK<borders=GCON_Territory>. Once the colony is
$LINK<connected=GCON_Trade> to one of your cities by road, your civilization can use that resource. A colony
uses up the worker that builds it, and disappears when your borders grow to take it in.
^
^See also $LINK<Build Colony=TFRM_Build_Colony>.

#GCON_Combat
Combat
^[Combat] happens when a unit attacks an enemy unit. The attacker's attack strength is compared with the
defender's defense strength, after the defender's bonuses for $LINK<terrain=GCON_Terrain_Combat>, fortification,
fortresses, city walls and city size are added. The battle is fought in rounds: in each round one side loses a hit
point, and the stronger a unit is relative to its foe, the more rounds it wins. The battle ends when one unit has
no hit points left. How many hit points a unit has depends on its $LINK<experience=GCON_Experience>.
^
^{Retreat}
^A fast unit, one that moves two or more squares a turn, may withdraw from a battle it is losing, unless its
opponent is also fast.
^
^{Bombardment}
^Artillery, ships and aircraft with a bombard strength can strike a square from a distance without moving
into it. Bombardment can damage units, and in cities it can kill citizens or destroy buildings, but it never
kills a unit outright unless the bombarding unit's bombardment is lethal.
^
^{Healing}
^A damaged unit heals by resting a turn without moving. It heals fastest in a city, and does not heal in enemy
territory unless its civilization has $LINK<Battlefield Medicine=BLDG_Battlefield_Medicine>. A
$LINK<Barracks=BLDG_Barracks>, $LINK<Harbor=BLDG_Harbor> or $LINK<Airport=BLDG_Airport> heals land, sea or air
units completely in a single turn.

#GCON_Commerce
Commerce
^[Commerce] measures the trade, travel and exchange of ideas in the land around a city. It comes from the
squares a city works: from roads, from squares beside rivers, from water squares, and from some
$LINK<resources=GCON_ResourcesN>. Some is lost to $LINK<corruption=GCON_Corruption>.
^
^The rest is divided, by the rates you set, between three uses:
^* gold for the $LINK<treasury=GCON_Treasury>,
^* $LINK<research=GCON_Research> into new advances, and
^* luxuries, which make citizens $LINK<happy=GCON_Happy_Faces>.
^
^A $LINK<Republic=GOVT_Republic> or $LINK<Democracy=GOVT_Democracy> adds one commerce to every worked square
that already makes some.

#GCON_Conversion
City Defection
^A city can [defect], throwing off its rulers and joining another civilization. This is most likely for a city
with little culture that lies close to a civilization with a much greater $LINK<culture=GCON_Culture>.
^
^A city is more likely to defect when it is in $LINK<civil disorder=GCON_Disorder>, when it holds citizens of the
other civilization's $LINK<nationality=GCON_Nationality>, when it is far from your capital and near theirs, and
when it is the target of enemy $LINK<propaganda=GCON_Espionage>. Military units in the city,
$LINK<a celebration=GCON_We_Love_The_King_Day> and a $LINK<Courthouse=BLDG_Courthouse> make it less likely.

#GCON_Collateral_Damage
Collateral Damage
^Some units can cause [collateral damage]: when they attack a square, the fighting may also wreck buildings
or population there, not just the defending unit.

#GCON_Corruption
Corruption and Waste
^[Corruption] is commerce lost to graft and theft, and [waste] is production lost to inefficiency. Both are
taken from each city before the rest is used.
^
^{What makes them worse}
^* Distance from your capital: cities far from the $LINK<Palace=BLDG_Palace> lose much more.
^* The size of your empire: the more cities you own beyond the number your government can run well, the worse
corruption gets everywhere.
^* Your $LINK<government=GCON_Governments>: corruption ranges from catastrophic under
$LINK<Anarchy=GOVT_Anarchy> to minimal under $LINK<Democracy=GOVT_Democracy>. Under
$LINK<Communism=GOVT_Communism> it is the same in every city, however far away.
^
^{What reduces them}
^* A $LINK<Courthouse=BLDG_Courthouse> or $LINK<Police Station=BLDG_Police_Station> in the city.
^* A $LINK<Forbidden Palace=BLDG_Forbidden_Palace>, which acts as a second capital for nearby cities.
^* Police officers, a kind of $LINK<specialist=GCON_Specialists>.
^* A road, harbor or airport link to your capital.
^* The $LINK<Commercial=GCON_Strengths> civilization strength.

#GCON_Craters
Craters
^[Craters] are left behind by heavy bombardment and by nuclear attacks. Like
$LINK<pollution=GCON_Pollution>, a crater lowers the food, shields and commerce of its square by one until a
$LINK<worker=PRTO_Worker> clears it with $LINK<Clear Damage=TFRM_Clear_Damage>.

#GCON_Culture
Culture
^[Culture] is the lasting influence of your civilization's arts, faith and learning. Temples, libraries,
wonders and other buildings earn culture points every turn for their city, and the points pile up over the
whole game. Buildings that have stood for a long time earn more.
^
^{Borders}
^A city's $LINK<borders=GCON_Territory> grow as its culture total passes 10, 100, 1,000 and 10,000 points.
Where two cities' borders meet, the one with more culture claims the disputed squares.
^
^{National Culture}
^The total culture of all your cities shapes how other civilizations see you, whether conquered cities
$LINK<resist=GCON_Resistance> you, and whether your cities $LINK<defect=GCON_Conversion> or attract others'.
Great enough culture can also win the game; see $LINK<Victory Conditions=GCON_Victory>.

#GCON_Disease
Disease
^Some lands are unhealthy. Units that stay in $LINK<flood plains=TERR_Flood_Plain>, $LINK<jungle=TERR_Jungle>
or $LINK<marsh=TERR_Marsh> can lose hit points to [disease], and cities among them may lose citizens. Clearing
jungle and marsh removes the danger, and the risk in flood plains ends with $LINK<Sanitation=TECH_Sanitation>.

#GCON_Disorder
Civil Disorder
^A city falls into [civil disorder] when it has more unhappy citizens than happy ones. While it riots, the city
produces nothing: no shields, no taxes or research, and no food is stored.
^
^A city left in disorder too long may lose buildings to the mob or $LINK<defect=GCON_Conversion> to another
civilization, and widespread disorder under some governments can bring down the government itself. To end
disorder, raise the luxury rate, turn citizens into entertainers, station military police where your
government allows it, or build what makes people content; see $LINK<Citizen Moods=GCON_Moods>.

#GCON_Draft
Draft
^Once $LINK<Nationalism=TECH_Nationalism> is known, a city can [draft] some of its citizens into the army. Each
draft turns one citizen into a conscript of the best defensive foot unit you can build, such as a
$LINK<Rifleman=PRTO_Rifleman> or $LINK<Infantry=PRTO_Infantry>.
^
^How many citizens a city may draft each turn depends on your government:
^Anarchy	0
^Despotism, Monarchy, Feudalism, Communism, Fascism	2
^Republic, Democracy	1
^
^Drafting angers the city's people, and the unhappiness lasts for many turns.

#GCON_Embassies
Embassies
^An [embassy] in another civilization's capital lets you see that civilization's advances, treasury,
cities and agreements, and opens the way to $LINK<diplomatic agreements=GCON_Agreements>. Embassies can be
established once $LINK<Writing=TECH_Writing> is known, and cost gold.

#GCON_Enslavement
Enslavement
^A unit that can [enslave] sometimes captures the units it defeats instead of destroying them. After each battle
it wins there is a one in three chance that the loser is turned into a unit of the winner's own, such as a
$LINK<Worker=PRTO_Worker>.
^
^The Mayan $LINK<Javelin Thrower=PRTO_Javelin_Thrower>, the $LINK<Privateer=PRTO_Privateer> and the English
$LINK<Man-O-War=PRTO_Man-O-War> can enslave.

#GCON_Espionage
Espionage
^[Espionage] lets your agents learn about other civilizations and work against them in secret. Some
diplomatic missions, like investigating a city, are possible soon after $LINK<embassies=GCON_Embassies> become
available. Once you know $LINK<Espionage=TECH_Espionage> and have built the
$LINK<Intelligence Agency=BLDG_Intelligence_Center>, you can plant spies and carry out spy missions.
^
^Missions cost gold, and the more you spend the better the chance of success. A failed mission can be traced
back to you and damage your relations. See $LINK<Espionage Missions=GCON_Espionage_Missions>.

#GCON_Espionage_Missions
Espionage Missions
^{Establish Embassy}
^Opens an embassy in the target's capital.
^{Investigate City}
^Shows you everything in one of the target's cities.
^{Plant Spy}
^Places a spy in the target's capital, which the missions below need.
^{Steal Technology}
^Takes an advance the target knows and you do not.
^{Steal Plans}
^Reveals where the target's military units are.
^{Steal World Map}
^Copies the target's $LINK<map=GCON_Maps> of the world.
^{Propaganda}
^Stirs up a target city in the hope that it $LINK<defects=GCON_Conversion> to you.
^{Sabotage Production}
^Destroys the shields a target city has put toward what it is building.
^{Expose Enemy Spy}
^Tries to uncover a spy planted in one of your own cities.

#GCON_Experience
Combat Experience
^Every military unit has an [experience] level, which sets how many hit points it has, and so how much damage
it can take before it is destroyed.
^
^Conscript	2 hit points
^Regular	3 hit points
^Veteran	4 hit points
^Elite	5 hit points
^
^Cities build regular units, or veterans if they have a $LINK<Barracks=BLDG_Barracks> (for land units),
$LINK<Harbor=BLDG_Harbor> (for ships) or $LINK<Airport=BLDG_Airport> (for aircraft). Drafted units are
conscripts.
^
^{Promotion}
^A unit that wins a battle may be promoted to the next level: a conscript has a one in two chance, a regular
one in four, and a veteran one in eight. $LINK<Militaristic=GCON_Strengths> civilizations are promoted more
often. An elite unit that wins a battle may give rise to a $LINK<leader=GCON_Leaders>.

#GCON_Food
Food
^Every citizen eats two [food] each turn. The food a city's worked squares produce beyond that is stored, and
when the store is full the city grows by one citizen. A city that cannot feed its people draws on its store, and
when the store runs out a citizen starves.
^
^The store a city must fill to grow is larger for bigger cities. A $LINK<Granary=BLDG_Granary> keeps half the
food when the city grows, so the next citizen comes sooner.
^
^Food comes mostly from $LINK<grassland=TERR_Grassland>, $LINK<flood plains=TERR_Flood_Plain>, water squares
and food $LINK<resources=GCON_ResourcesN>, and $LINK<irrigation=TFRM_Irrigation> adds one more on flat land.
^
^{Growth limits}
^A city cannot grow beyond size 6 without an $LINK<Aqueduct=BLDG_Aqueduct>, or beyond 12 without a
$LINK<Hospital=BLDG_Hospital>.

#GCON_Fortresses
Fortresses
^A [fortress] is a strongpoint that a $LINK<worker=PRTO_Worker> can build once
$LINK<Construction=TECH_Construction> is known. Units defending in a fortress get +50% defense, and a fortress
exerts a $LINK<zone of control=GCON_ZOC>. A fortress can later be strengthened into a
$LINK<barricade=GCON_Barricades>.

#GCON_Golden_Age
Golden Age
^A [Golden Age] is a period of 20 turns of plenty in which every worked square that already produces at least
one shield or one commerce produces one more. A civilization has only one Golden Age in a game.
^
^{What starts it}
^* Its special unit winning a battle against another civilization (beating barbarians does not count).
^* Owning Great Wonders that between them match both of its $LINK<strengths=GCON_Strengths>. Each wonder's page
says which strengths it belongs to.

#GCON_Governments
Governments
^Your [government] decides much about how your civilization runs:
^* how much $LINK<corruption and waste=GCON_Corruption> cities suffer,
^* how quickly $LINK<workers=PRTO_Worker> work,
^* how you can $LINK<hurry production=GCON_Hurry_Production>,
^* how many units it supports for free, and what the others cost (see $LINK<Unit Support=GCON_Unit_Support>),
^* how many units can act as military police, and how many citizens a city can $LINK<draft=GCON_Draft>,
^* whether citizens grow $LINK<weary of war=GCON_War_Weariness>.
^
^The governments are $LINK<Anarchy=GOVT_Anarchy>, $LINK<Despotism=GOVT_Despotism>,
$LINK<Monarchy=GOVT_Monarchy>, $LINK<Feudalism=GOVT_Feudalism>, $LINK<Republic=GOVT_Republic>,
$LINK<Communism=GOVT_Communism>, $LINK<Fascism=GOVT_Fascism> and $LINK<Democracy=GOVT_Democracy>. Every
civilization starts under Despotism, and learns the others through advances. To change government you need a
$LINK<revolution=GCON_Revolution>.

#GCON_Happy_Faces
Happy and Content Faces
^Many things in a city produce [content faces] or [happy faces]:
^* A content face turns one unhappy citizen content.
^* A happy face turns one content citizen happy.
^
^Temples, cathedrals, colosseums, many wonders, and military police give content faces. Luxury
$LINK<resources=GCON_ResourcesL>, the luxury rate and entertainers give happy faces. When there are more faces of
one kind than citizens they can change, the extra faces still help, but less.
^
^See also $LINK<Citizen Moods=GCON_Moods>.

#GCON_Hurry_Production
Hurry Production
^A city can [hurry] what it is building and finish it at once, at a price that depends on how much work is
left. Hurrying costs more when nothing has yet been put toward the project. How you pay depends on your
$LINK<government=GCON_Governments>:
^
^{Paid labor}
^Under $LINK<Monarchy=GOVT_Monarchy>, the $LINK<Republic=GOVT_Republic> and $LINK<Democracy=GOVT_Democracy>,
you pay gold from the $LINK<treasury=GCON_Treasury>.
^
^{Forced labor}
^Under $LINK<Despotism=GOVT_Despotism>, $LINK<Feudalism=GOVT_Feudalism>, $LINK<Communism=GOVT_Communism> and
$LINK<Fascism=GOVT_Fascism>, citizens are worked to death: the city loses population, and the survivors stay
resentful for many turns.
^
^Nothing can be hurried under $LINK<Anarchy=GOVT_Anarchy>, and wonders cannot be hurried with gold or
citizens. A $LINK<leader=GCON_Leaders> can instead finish a project at once.

#GCON_Keypad
Number Keypad
^With Num Lock on, the number keypad moves the selected unit, each key pointing the way it lies from the
center key:
^7 northwest	8 north	9 northeast
^4 west		6 east
^1 southwest	2 south	3 southeast
^
^The arrow keys move units north, south, east and west.

#GCON_Landmark_Terrain
Landmark Terrain
^A scenario may mark some squares as [landmark terrain]: a special version of an ordinary terrain type with its
own food, shield or commerce values. Random maps do not have landmark terrain.

#GCON_Leaders
Leaders
^Great [leaders] are rare figures who can change the course of a game.
^
^{Military Leaders}
^A military leader can appear when one of your $LINK<elite=GCON_Experience> units wins a battle; the
$LINK<Heroic Epic=BLDG_Epic> makes this more likely. A military leader can form an $LINK<army=GCON_Armies>,
or finish building a city improvement or small wonder at once. Either way he is used up.
^
^{Scientific Leaders}
^A scientific leader can appear when your civilization is the first in the world to learn an advance. He can
start an $LINK<Age of Science=GCON_Age_of_Science> or finish any building in a city, even a Great Wonder.
^
^A leader cannot fight, and is lost if an enemy reaches him alone.

#GCON_Locked_Alliances
Locked Alliances
^A scenario may group civilizations into [locked alliances] that last all game. Allies in a locked alliance
cannot attack one another, and an attack on one is an attack on all. A scenario may also set two alliances at
permanent war, and may let an alliance win together.

#GCON_Maintenance
Maintenance
^Most city improvements cost gold every turn to keep running. This [maintenance] is paid from the
$LINK<treasury=GCON_Treasury>; each improvement's page shows its cost. If the treasury runs dry, buildings are
sold off to pay the bills. $LINK<Smith's Trading Company=BLDG_Trading_Company> pays the upkeep of trade
buildings for you.

#GCON_Maps
Maps
^The world starts hidden. Your units and cities reveal the squares around them, and areas you have seen but
cannot see now are shown as you last saw them.
^
^Your civilization's map of the world can be traded to other civilizations, or bought from them, at the
negotiating table. A spy can also steal one; see $LINK<Espionage Missions=GCON_Espionage_Missions>.

#GCON_Mobilization
Mobilization
^Once $LINK<Nationalism=TECH_Nationalism> is known, you can put your economy on a war footing by
[mobilizing]. While mobilized, cities building military units get one extra shield from every worked square that
already produces shields, but most peacetime improvements cannot be built. Once you mobilize, you cannot return
to normal until you are at peace.

#GCON_Moods
Citizen Moods
^Each citizen is [happy], [content] or [unhappy]. A city with more unhappy citizens than happy ones falls into
$LINK<civil disorder=GCON_Disorder>.
^
^{What makes people unhappy}
^* Crowding: only the first few citizens of each city are born content; see
$LINK<Overpopulation=GCON_Overpopulation>.
^* $LINK<War weariness=GCON_War_Weariness>.
^* $LINK<Forced labor=GCON_Hurry_Production> and the $LINK<draft=GCON_Draft>.
^
^{What makes people happier}
^* Buildings: $LINK<Temple=BLDG_Temple>, $LINK<Colosseum=BLDG_Colosseum>, $LINK<Cathedral=BLDG_Cathedral>.
^* Wonders such as $LINK<The Hanging Gardens=BLDG_Hanging_Gardens> and
$LINK<JS Bach's Cathedral=BLDG_Grand_Cathedral>.
^* Luxury $LINK<resources=GCON_ResourcesL> and the luxury rate.
^* Entertainers; see $LINK<Specialists=GCON_Specialists>.
^* Military police: under some governments, units in a city keep order.
^
^See also $LINK<Happy and Content Faces=GCON_Happy_Faces>.

#GCON_Nationality
Nationality
^Every citizen has a [nationality]: the civilization he was born into. When you take a city, its people keep
their old nationality and become [foreign nationals]. They work normally in peacetime, but while you are at war
with their homeland they are more easily made unhappy and more open to its
$LINK<propaganda=GCON_Espionage>. Over time they $LINK<assimilate=GCON_Assimilation>. Captured workers keep
their nationality too.

#GCON_Outposts
Outposts
^An [outpost] is a watchtower that a $LINK<worker=PRTO_Worker> can build once $LINK<Masonry=TECH_Masonry> is
known, on a land square in your own territory or in no one's. It keeps watch over the squares around it, seeing
further from hills and mountains. Building it uses up the worker. An outpost belongs only to its builder, and
is destroyed if it falls inside another civilization's borders.

#GCON_Overpopulation
Overpopulation
^Only the first few citizens of each city are born content; every citizen beyond them is born unhappy.
How many are content depends on the difficulty level:
^Chieftain	4
^Warlord	3
^Regent	2
^Monarch	2
^Emperor	1
^Demigod	1
^Deity	1
^Creator	1
^
^Having many more cities than your government can run well also makes citizens unhappy. See
$LINK<Citizen Moods=GCON_Moods>.

#GCON_Plants
Power Plants
^A city with a $LINK<Factory=BLDG_Factory> can add a [power plant], which increases the factory's bonus. A city
can have only one power plant; building a new one replaces the old.
^$LINK<Coal Plant=BLDG_Coal_Plant>	more production, but pollutes
^$LINK<Hydro Plant=BLDG_Hydro_Plant>	no pollution; needs a river
^$LINK<Nuclear Plant=BLDG_Nuclear_Plant>	the largest bonus, but can melt down
^$LINK<Solar Plant=BLDG_Solar_Plant>	no pollution
^
^$LINK<Hoover Dam=BLDG_Hoover_Dam> gives every city on its continent a Hydro Plant.

#GCON_Plague
Plague
^Some scenarios include [plague], which can strike cities and kill citizens and units. Large, walled and
well-connected cities suffer most, and $LINK<Sanitation=TECH_Sanitation> greatly reduces its effect.

#GCON_Pollution
Pollution
^[Pollution] fouls the land around industrial cities. Factories and power plants, large populations and
nuclear accidents all create it. A polluted square produces less until a $LINK<worker=PRTO_Worker> cleans it up
with $LINK<Clear Damage=TFRM_Clear_Damage>.
^
^If too much of the world is left polluted, the climate begins to change, and land all over the world grows
poorer: forests die off and fertile land dries out.
^
^A $LINK<Mass Transit System=BLDG_Mass_Transit_System> stops pollution caused by population, and a
$LINK<Recycling Center=BLDG_Recycling_Center> cuts pollution from production. Clean power plants help too.

#GCON_Radar_Towers
Radar Towers
^A [radar tower] can be built by a $LINK<worker=PRTO_Worker> inside your territory once
$LINK<Advanced Flight=TECH_Advanced_Flight> is known. Your units within two squares of it get +25% on attack and
defense. Several towers covering the same square do not add up. Building one uses up the worker, and it is
destroyed if it falls inside another civilization's borders.

#GCON_Radius
City Radius
^A city's citizens work the squares around it, up to two squares away. Only squares inside your own
$LINK<borders=GCON_Territory> can be worked, so a new city may at first reach fewer squares. Each worked square
yields $LINK<food=GCON_Food>, $LINK<shields=GCON_Shields> and $LINK<commerce=GCON_Commerce>; the city's own
square is always worked for free.

#GCON_Research
Scientific Research
^Your scientists are always working toward a new [advance]. Each turn the research share of your cities'
$LINK<commerce=GCON_Commerce>, set by the science rate, goes toward the advance you have chosen. When enough has
been gathered, the advance is learned and you choose the next.
^
^To research faster, raise the science rate, build $LINK<Libraries=BLDG_Library>,
$LINK<Universities=BLDG_University> and $LINK<Research Labs=BLDG_Lab>, use scientists (see
$LINK<Specialists=GCON_Specialists>), or build wonders such as
$LINK<Copernicus' Observatory=BLDG_Solar_System> and $LINK<Newton's University=BLDG_Great_University>.
Advances that other civilizations you know already have are cheaper to research.

#GCON_Resistance
Resistance
^When you capture a city, some of its people may refuse to accept your rule. These [resisters] do no work, and
the city produces nothing until they give up. The greater the conquered civilization's
$LINK<culture=GCON_Culture> compared with yours, the more resisters there are. Stationing more units in the city,
or making peace, makes them give up sooner.

#GCON_ResourcesN

#GCON_Revolution
Revolution
^To change your $LINK<government=GCON_Governments> you must start a [revolution]. Your civilization then falls
into $LINK<Anarchy=GOVT_Anarchy> for a few turns before the new government takes over.
$LINK<Religious=GCON_Strengths> civilizations get through anarchy faster. Long civil disorder can also start a
revolution you did not ask for.

#GCON_Ruins
Ruins
^When a city is destroyed, [ruins] mark the spot. They do not change what the square produces, and a new city
can be founded on top of them.

#GCON_Shields
Production and Shields
^[Shields] measure a city's production: raw materials and the labor to work them. The shields from a city's
worked squares, less $LINK<waste=GCON_Corruption>, go toward whatever the city is building, and when enough have
been gathered it is finished.
^
^Shields come mostly from $LINK<hills=TERR_Hills>, $LINK<mountains=TERR_Mountains>,
$LINK<forests=TERR_Forest> and $LINK<mines=TFRM_Mine>, and from some $LINK<resources=GCON_ResourcesN>. A
$LINK<Factory=BLDG_Factory> and $LINK<power plants=GCON_Plants> multiply a city's production.
^
^A city with nothing to build can turn its production into gold; see $LINK<Wealth=BLDG_Wealth>.

#GCON_Short_Game_Modes
Short Game Modes
^Some games use special rules that can end them quickly.
^
^{Regicide}
^Each civilization has a king unit. If its king is killed, the civilization is out of the game.
^
^{Mass Regicide}
^Each civilization has several kings, and is out of the game only when all are lost.
^
^{Elimination}
^A civilization is out of the game as soon as it loses a city.
^
^{Capture the Princess}
^Each civilization has a $LINK<Princess=PRTO_Princess>. Capturing another's princess and bringing her home
scores victory points.
^
^{Victory Locations}
^Holding certain squares at the end of each turn scores victory points.
^
^When a civilization is eliminated in these modes, its cities and units are destroyed and leave
$LINK<ruins=GCON_Ruins>.

#GCON_Specialists
Specialists
^A citizen who is not working a square can be made a [specialist]. Specialists produce nothing from the land,

^Entertainer	luxuries, to make citizens happy
^Tax Collector	gold
^Scientist	research

#GCON_Stealth_Attack
Stealth Attack
^A unit with [stealth attack] chooses which unit in an enemy stack to attack, instead of facing the stack's
best defender. $LINK<Submarines=PRTO_Submarine>, $LINK<Nuclear Submarines=PRTO_Nuclear_Submarine> and the

#GCON_Strengths
Civilization Strengths
^Every civilization has two of eight [strengths]. Each gives a lasting advantage, makes some city improvements
cost half as much, and helps decide which advances the civilization starts with and which Great Wonders can
start its $LINK<Golden Age=GCON_Golden_Age>.
^
^{Agricultural}
^The central square of each city produces an extra food. Improvements such as the
$LINK<Aqueduct=BLDG_Aqueduct> are cheaper.
^{Commercial}
^Less $LINK<corruption=GCON_Corruption>, and the central square of each larger city produces extra commerce.
Trade buildings such as the $LINK<Marketplace=BLDG_Marketplace> are cheaper.
^{Expansionist}
^Starts with a $LINK<Scout=PRTO_Scout> and can build more. Tribal villages are friendlier.
^{Industrious}
^$LINK<Workers=PRTO_Worker> work faster, and the central square of each larger city produces an extra shield.
^{Militaristic}
^Units are $LINK<promoted=GCON_Experience> more often, and military buildings such as the
$LINK<Barracks=BLDG_Barracks> are cheaper.
^{Religious}
^$LINK<Anarchy=GOVT_Anarchy> after a $LINK<revolution=GCON_Revolution> is short, and the
$LINK<Temple=BLDG_Temple> and $LINK<Cathedral=BLDG_Cathedral> are cheaper.
^{Scientific}
^A free advance at the start of each new era, and the $LINK<Library=BLDG_Library> and other science buildings
are cheaper.
^{Seafaring}
^Coastal cities produce extra commerce, ships move faster and are less likely to be lost at sea, and the
$LINK<Harbor=BLDG_Harbor> and other harbor buildings are cheaper.

^Marsh	+20%

^

#GCON_Territory
Territory
^A civilization's [territory] is the land inside its [borders], which are drawn on the map in its color. Each
city claims the land around it, and claims more as its $LINK<culture=GCON_Culture> grows.
^
^{Inside your borders}
^* Other civilizations cannot use your roads and railroads without a
$LINK<Right of Passage=GCON_Agreements>.
^* Resources need no $LINK<colony=GCON_Colony> to be used.
^* You can see everything that happens.
^* You can ask foreign units to leave, and units that stay without permission put the peace at risk.

#GCON_Tourist_Attraction
Tourist Attraction
^A Great Wonder that has stood for over a thousand years becomes a [tourist attraction]. Visitors bring gold to
its city every turn, and the older the wonder, the more they bring. Most of the ancient and medieval wonders,
and a few later ones, can become tourist attractions; their pages say so.

#GCON_Trade
Trade and Trade Routes
^Cities that are [connected] share every tradable $LINK<resource=GCON_ResourcesN> that reaches any of them.
Two cities are connected when:
^* a road or railroad runs between them,
^* both have a $LINK<Harbor=BLDG_Harbor> and your ships can safely sail between them, or
^* both have an $LINK<Airport=BLDG_Airport>.
^
^A link through enemy territory, or a harbor blockaded by enemy ships, does not count. Early ships can only
sail safely near the coast, so sea and ocean routes open up as your seafaring advances improve.
^
^{Trade with other civilizations}
^When your capital is connected to another civilization's capital, you can trade resources with it at the
negotiating table.

#GCON_Transport
Transport
^Some ships, and the $LINK<Helicopter=PRTO_Helicopter>, can carry other units. A land unit boards a ship by
moving onto it from the shore, or by being loaded in a port city, and gets off by moving onto land. A ship that
sinks takes everything aboard with it. Helicopters carry only foot soldiers.
^
^Most units cannot attack straight from a ship; [amphibious] units, such as $LINK<Marines=PRTO_Marine>,
can.

#GCON_Treasury
Treasury
^Your [treasury] holds your civilization's gold. Each turn the tax income of every city is added to it, and
your costs are paid out of it:
^* $LINK<maintenance=GCON_Maintenance> of city improvements,
^* $LINK<support=GCON_Unit_Support> for units beyond those your government supports for free,
^* payments promised in $LINK<agreements=GCON_Agreements>.
^
^Gold is also spent to $LINK<hurry production=GCON_Hurry_Production>, upgrade units, establish
$LINK<embassies=GCON_Embassies>, and carry out $LINK<espionage=GCON_Espionage>. If the treasury runs out,
buildings are sold to pay the bills. With $LINK<Wall Street=BLDG_Wall_Street>, the treasury earns interest.

#GCON_Victory

#GCON_Volcanic_Eruptions
Volcanic Eruptions
^A $LINK<volcano=TERR_Volcano> can [erupt] without warning, though it often smokes for a few turns first. An

#GCON_War_Weariness

$LINK<Democracy=GOVT_Democracy> grow [weary] of war. The longer a war lasts, and the more battles are lost,

$LINK<defect=GCON_Conversion> or fall for enemy propaganda.

#PRTO_Settler
^[Settlers] found new cities. Move a settler to a good site and order it to build a city; the settler becomes
the city's first citizens. A settler can also join an existing city, adding two citizens to it.
^
^Building a settler takes two citizens from the city that builds it, so a city must have at least three
citizens to build one. Settlers cannot fight and should be escorted where there is danger.

#DESC_PRTO_Settler
^Every civilization began with families who left home to farm new land. Groups of settlers carried seed, tools
and livestock into the wilderness, cleared fields, and raised the first houses of villages that might one day be
great cities.
^
^Organized colonization has shaped history again and again: Phoenician and Greek colonists spread around the
Mediterranean, medieval Germans moved east into Slavic lands, and in the nineteenth century millions of
homesteaders settled the plains of North America, Siberia and Australia.

#PRTO_Worker
^[Workers] improve the land: they build $LINK<roads=TFRM_Road>, $LINK<irrigation=TFRM_Irrigation> and
$LINK<mines=TFRM_Mine>, clear forests and wetlands, and clean up pollution. See
$LINK<Worker Actions=GCON_Worker_Jobs>.
^
^Building a worker takes one citizen from its city, and a worker can join a city to add one citizen back. Workers
cannot fight; an enemy unit that reaches an unguarded worker captures it.

#DESC_PRTO_Worker
^Before machines, nearly everything was built by human muscle. Laborers dug irrigation ditches along the Nile
and the Euphrates, terraced the hillsides of the Andes and China, and laid the stone roads that tied empires
together.
^
^Much of this labor was forced: conscripted peasants, serfs and slaves built the pyramids, the Great Wall and
countless roads and canals. Only in the last two centuries did steam, then gasoline and electricity, begin to
take over the heaviest work.

#PRTO_Scout
^[Scouts] are fast, unarmed explorers that move two squares a turn. They are best used early in the game to
explore the land and find tribal villages, other civilizations and good city sites. Only
$LINK<Expansionist=GCON_Strengths> civilizations can build scouts, and they start the game with one.

#DESC_PRTO_Scout
^Scouts went ahead of armies and migrating peoples to find water, game, passes and enemies. They traveled light
and fast, relied on stealth rather than weapons, and brought back what they had seen.
^
^Many peoples prized the skills of the scout: reading tracks, finding the way without maps and living off the
land. Long into the modern age, armies still sent scouts and pathfinders ahead of the main body.

#PRTO_Explorer
^[Explorers] are unarmed units that treat every square as if it had a road, so they cross mountains, forests
and jungles as easily as grassland. They are ideal for mapping the remaining wild places of a continent.

#DESC_PRTO_Explorer
^From the fifteenth century on, Europeans set out to map the parts of the world that were unknown to them.
Explorers such as Samuel de Champlain, Alexander Mackenzie, Lewis and Clark, and later David Livingstone pushed
far inland by river, on foot and on horseback.
^
^They depended heavily on local guides and peoples who had long known the land. Their reports and maps opened
the way for traders, missionaries and settlers, and often for conquest.

#PRTO_Warrior
^[Warriors] are the first military unit. They are weak and are soon outclassed, but are cheap and can be built
from the very start of the game, which makes them useful for exploring and for guarding new cities against
early threats.

#DESC_PRTO_Warrior
^The first fighters were simply the able-bodied men of a band or village, armed with clubs, stone axes and
spears. War in the Stone Age was mostly raiding: ambushes and quick attacks to take food, livestock or
captives, or to avenge a wrong.
^
^Warriors fought as individuals rather than in drilled formations, and personal courage counted for more than
discipline. Their equipment was whatever a hunter already owned.

#PRTO_Archer
^[Archers] are the first unit built for attack rather than defense. They are good for early raids and for
taking lightly defended cities.

#DESC_PRTO_Archer
^The bow is one of humanity's oldest weapons, used for tens of thousands of years by hunters before it was
turned against other people. It let a fighter strike from a distance, out of reach of clubs and spears.
^
^Every ancient army had its archers. Egyptian and Assyrian bowmen fought from chariots and on foot, and the
composite bow of horn, wood and sinew gave the peoples of the steppe a weapon of great power.

#PRTO_Spearman
^[Spearmen] are the first real defensive unit, and a good garrison for early cities.

#DESC_PRTO_Spearman
^A spear is easy to make and easy to learn, and for thousands of years it was the main weapon of foot soldiers.
Bronze spearheads made the weapon far more deadly.
^
^Spearmen fighting shoulder to shoulder in close ranks, their shields overlapping, could hold off attackers
who came at them one by one. Such formations were the backbone of armies in Sumer, Egypt and Greece.

#PRTO_Swordsman
^[Swordsmen] are strong attackers of the Iron Age, able to take cities defended by spearmen and archers. Building
them needs $LINK<iron=GOOD_Iron>.

#DESC_PRTO_Swordsman
^Swords appeared in the Bronze Age, but bronze was costly and soft. When iron working spread after about 1200
BC, good blades became cheap enough to arm whole armies.
^
^A swordsman fought at close quarters, where a short blade and a large shield gave him the edge over a man with a
spear. The Roman legions, armed with the short gladius, showed how effective trained swordsmen could be.

#PRTO_Chariot
^[Chariots] are fast attackers that move two squares a turn, and can withdraw from a losing battle against slower
units. Being wheeled, they cannot enter mountains, volcanoes, jungle or marsh unless following a road. They need
$LINK<horses=GOOD_Horses>.

#DESC_PRTO_Chariot
^The light, spoked-wheel chariot was developed around 2000 BC on the steppes and soon spread across the Near
East, Egypt, India and China. Pulled by two horses, it carried a driver and an archer or spearman.
^
^For several centuries chariots were the most feared weapon on the battlefield, and the great powers of the
time, Egypt and the Hittites among them, fielded them by the thousand. Kadesh, fought around 1274 BC, was one of
the largest chariot battles in history.

#PRTO_Horseman
^[Horsemen] are the first mounted unit. They move two squares a turn and can withdraw from a losing battle
against slower units. They need $LINK<horses=GOOD_Horses>.

#DESC_PRTO_Horseman
^Horses were tamed on the steppes of Central Asia, but riding them in battle came later than harnessing them to
chariots. By the first millennium BC, riders armed with bows and javelins had become a powerful force.
^
^The Scythians and other steppe peoples were the first great horsemen, and their speed let them strike, scatter
and strike again. Settled peoples soon raised cavalry of their own.

#PRTO_Pikeman
^[Pikemen] are the strong defenders of the Middle Ages, a solid garrison against the knights and swordsmen of the
age. They need $LINK<iron=GOOD_Iron>.

#DESC_PRTO_Pikeman
^The pike was a spear up to six meters long. Held by men packed in deep blocks, it presented a hedge of points
that horses would not charge.
^
^Macedonian phalanxes used a similar weapon, but pikemen became decisive again in the late Middle Ages, when
Scottish and Swiss infantry showed that disciplined foot soldiers could defeat armored knights.

#PRTO_Medieval_Infantry
^[Medieval Infantry] are the heavy foot soldiers of the Middle Ages, better in attack than the swordsmen before
them. They need $LINK<iron=GOOD_Iron>.

#DESC_PRTO_Medieval_Infantry
^In medieval Europe most battles were decided on foot, even by knights who dismounted to fight. Men-at-arms
in mail and plate, armed with swords, axes, maces and polearms, formed the core of many armies.
^
^Towns raised militias of armed citizens, and kings hired professional soldiers. By the end of the period,
well-led infantry had again become the most important part of an army.

#PRTO_Longbowman
^[Longbowmen] are powerful foot attackers of the Middle Ages, and need no resources to build.

#DESC_PRTO_Longbowman
^The longbow, a simple bow of yew as tall as a man, needed great strength and years of practice to draw. In skilled
hands it could shoot many arrows a minute and pierce mail at short range.
^
^English and Welsh longbowmen won famous victories over the French at Crecy, Poitiers and Agincourt during the
Hundred Years' War. Firearms eventually replaced the longbow because a musketeer could be trained far more
quickly.

#PRTO_Knight
^[Knights] are armored horsemen, strong in attack and decent in defense. They move two squares a turn and can
withdraw from a losing battle against slower units. They need $LINK<horses=GOOD_Horses> and
$LINK<iron=GOOD_Iron>.

#DESC_PRTO_Knight
^The knight was a heavily armored mounted warrior who held land in return for military service. With the
stirrup, a high saddle and a couched lance, his charge could break most foot soldiers.
^
^Knights dominated European warfare from the eleventh to the fourteenth century, and knighthood became a whole
way of life with its own code of chivalry. Longbows, pikes and finally firearms ended their rule of the
battlefield.

#PRTO_Musketman
^[Musketmen] are the first gunpowder unit, and much stronger defenders than anything before them. They need
$LINK<saltpeter=GOOD_Saltpeter>.

#DESC_PRTO_Musketman
^Muskets, smoothbore guns fired by a burning match and later by a flint, spread through European armies in the
sixteenth century. A musket was slow to load and inaccurate, but any recruit could learn to use one in weeks.
^
^At first musketeers were protected by pikemen; with the invention of the bayonet in the late seventeenth
century, every infantryman could defend himself, and the pike disappeared.

#PRTO_Rifleman
^[Riflemen] are strong defenders of the early Industrial Age. They need no resources to build.

#DESC_PRTO_Rifleman
^A rifle has spiral grooves in its barrel that spin the bullet, making it far more accurate than a musket. Rifles
were long too slow to load for most soldiers, until new bullets in the mid-nineteenth century made them as quick
to load as muskets.
^
^Rifled weapons turned the open charge into slaughter, as the American Civil War and the wars of German
unification showed. Infantry learned to spread out, take cover and dig in.

#PRTO_Cavalry
^[Cavalry] are fast, hard-hitting mounted troops that move three squares a turn and have a
$LINK<zone of control=GCON_ZOC>. They can withdraw from a losing battle against slower units. They need
$LINK<horses=GOOD_Horses> and $LINK<saltpeter=GOOD_Saltpeter>.

#DESC_PRTO_Cavalry
^With the knight gone, horsemen became light and fast troops armed with sabers, pistols and carbines. Napoleonic
armies used massed cavalry to scout, to charge broken infantry and to pursue a beaten enemy.
^
^Cavalry stayed important through the nineteenth century, but machine guns and barbed wire made mounted charges
suicidal in the First World War, and horses gave way to trucks and tanks.

#PRTO_Guerilla
^[Guerillas] are irregular fighters, equally good in attack and defense. They need no resources, so any
civilization can raise them.

#DESC_PRTO_Guerilla
^Guerrilla means "little war" in Spanish, a name that came from the Spanish resistance to Napoleon's armies. Small
bands of fighters who know the land strike by surprise and melt away before a stronger enemy can respond.
^
^Guerrilla wars were fought across the twentieth century, from China to Algeria and Vietnam, and they often wore
down far larger and better-armed forces.

#PRTO_Infantry
^[Infantry] are the riflemen of the age of world wars, very strong in defense. They need
$LINK<rubber=GOOD_Rubber>.

#DESC_PRTO_Infantry
^The soldiers of the world wars carried bolt-action and later semi-automatic rifles, supported by machine
guns, mortars and grenades. They fought from trenches and foxholes and moved by rail and truck.
^
^Mass conscription put millions of men in uniform. Despite tanks and aircraft, infantry did most of the fighting
and suffered most of the losses.

#PRTO_Marine
^[Marines] are amphibious troops: they can attack straight from a ship onto a coastal square, even an enemy
city. They need $LINK<rubber=GOOD_Rubber>.

#DESC_PRTO_Marine
^Marines are soldiers trained to fight from ships. Many navies kept them for centuries as shipboard troops and
landing parties.
^
^In the Second World War, amphibious assault became an art. Landings in the Pacific and on the beaches of
Normandy needed special landing craft, close support from warships and aircraft, and troops trained to fight their
way ashore.

#PRTO_WWII_Paratrooper
^[Paratroopers] can be dropped from the air: from a city with an $LINK<airport=BLDG_Airport> they can make an
airdrop onto a square within range. Otherwise they fight as good defensive infantry, and they have a
$LINK<zone of control=GCON_ZOC>. They need $LINK<oil=GOOD_Oil> and $LINK<rubber=GOOD_Rubber>.

#DESC_PRTO_WWII_Paratrooper
^Parachute troops were first tried in the 1930s, and Germany used them to seize Belgian forts and Dutch airfields
in 1940 and to take Crete in 1941. The Allies followed with large airborne divisions.
^
^Paratroopers were lightly armed and had to win quickly or be relieved, since they could not easily be supplied.
Airborne landings in Normandy and the Netherlands in 1944 were among the largest ever made.

#PRTO_Paratrooper
^[Modern Paratroopers] are well-equipped airborne troops. From a city with an $LINK<airport=BLDG_Airport> they
can make an airdrop onto a square within range, and they are strong defenders with a
$LINK<zone of control=GCON_ZOC>. They need $LINK<oil=GOOD_Oil> and $LINK<rubber=GOOD_Rubber>.

#DESC_PRTO_Paratrooper
^Today's airborne forces carry far more firepower than their Second World War predecessors, including light
vehicles, anti-tank missiles and artillery that can be dropped with them.
^
^Helicopters and transport aircraft let them reach trouble spots quickly, and they often serve as a rapid
reaction force, the first troops sent to a crisis.

#PRTO_Mech_Infantry
^[Mech Infantry] ride into battle in armored vehicles. They are the strongest defenders, move two squares a turn
and have a $LINK<zone of control=GCON_ZOC>. They need $LINK<oil=GOOD_Oil> and $LINK<rubber=GOOD_Rubber>.

#DESC_PRTO_Mech_Infantry
^Mechanized infantry travel in armored personnel carriers or infantry fighting vehicles, which protect them
from shell fragments and small arms and let them keep up with tanks.
^
^Since the Second World War, combined forces of tanks and mechanized infantry have formed the striking power of
modern armies, each covering the other's weaknesses.

#PRTO_TOW_Infantry
^[TOW Infantry] are modern foot soldiers armed with guided anti-tank missiles. They are very strong defenders,
and need no resources to build.

#DESC_PRTO_TOW_Infantry
^TOW stands for "tube-launched, optically tracked, wire-guided", an American anti-tank missile first used in
combat in Vietnam in 1972. The gunner keeps his sight on the target, and the missile steers itself there along
a trailing wire.
^
^Such missiles let a few infantrymen destroy tanks costing many times more, and changed how armored forces
fight.

#PRTO_Tank
^[Tanks] are heavily armored attackers that move two squares a turn. They can attack more than once in a turn
(blitz), have a $LINK<zone of control=GCON_ZOC>, and can withdraw from a losing battle against slower units. They
need $LINK<oil=GOOD_Oil> and $LINK<rubber=GOOD_Rubber>.

#DESC_PRTO_Tank
^The tank was invented by the British during the First World War to cross trenches and barbed wire under machine
gun fire. The first ones, at the Somme in 1916, were slow and unreliable.
^
^Between the wars, faster and better-armed tanks were developed, and in the Second World War massed armored
divisions, supported by aircraft, could break through a front and race far behind it.

#PRTO_Modern_Armor
^[Modern Armor] is the most powerful land attacker. It moves three squares a turn, can attack more than once in a
turn (blitz), has a $LINK<zone of control=GCON_ZOC>, and can withdraw from a losing battle against slower units.
It needs $LINK<oil=GOOD_Oil> and $LINK<rubber=GOOD_Rubber>.

#DESC_PRTO_Modern_Armor
^Modern main battle tanks combine heavy composite armor, a powerful stabilized gun that can fire accurately on
the move, night sights and computer fire control.
^
^Tanks such as the American M1 Abrams, the German Leopard 2 and the Russian T-90 weigh fifty to seventy tons yet
can travel faster than sixty kilometers an hour across country.

#PRTO_Flak
^[Flak] guns are mobile anti-aircraft defenses that protect a stack of units or a city from enemy air attack.

#DESC_PRTO_Flak
^Flak comes from the German word for anti-aircraft gun. Fast-firing guns that sent shells bursting at the height
of enemy bombers were widely used in both world wars.
^
^In the Second World War, cities and factories were ringed with flak batteries guided by searchlights and,
later, radar. Few bombers were shot down by any one gun, but the barrage forced them to fly higher and bomb less
accurately.

#PRTO_Mobile_SAM
^[Mobile SAM] launchers protect units and cities against air attack, and move two squares a turn to keep up with
the army.

#DESC_PRTO_Mobile_SAM
^Surface-to-air missiles, first fielded in the 1950s, can reach aircraft far higher and faster than guns can.
Mounted on vehicles, they can follow an army and set up quickly.
^
^Mobile missile batteries shot down many aircraft over Vietnam and in the 1973 Arab-Israeli war, forcing air
forces to develop new tactics and electronic countermeasures.

#PRTO_Catapult
^[Catapults] are siege engines that bombard a neighboring square, wearing down defenders and damaging cities.
They cannot attack or defend on their own, so they need an escort. Being wheeled, they cannot enter mountains,
volcanoes, jungle or marsh unless following a road.

#DESC_PRTO_Catapult
^The catapult was invented in the Greek city of Syracuse around 400 BC. Early models worked like giant crossbows;
later ones used twisted ropes of sinew or hair to hurl stones and bolts.
^
^Alexander's engineers and the Roman army used catapults to batter city walls and to sweep defenders from the
battlements before an assault.

#PRTO_Trebuchet
^[Trebuchets] are siege engines of the Middle Ages, stronger than catapults. They bombard a neighboring square
and need an escort, since they cannot attack or defend on their own. Being wheeled, they cannot enter mountains,
volcanoes, jungle or marsh unless following a road.

#DESC_PRTO_Trebuchet
^The trebuchet is a great throwing arm on a pivot. The counterweight trebuchet, which spread through Europe and
the Middle East in the twelfth century, used a heavy box of earth or stones to swing the arm and could throw
stones weighing more than a hundred kilograms.
^
^Trebuchets could smash the strongest stone walls of their day, until gunpowder artillery replaced them in the
fifteenth century.

#PRTO_Cannon
^[Cannons] are gunpowder artillery that bombard a neighboring square. They need an escort, since they cannot
attack or defend on their own, and being wheeled they cannot enter mountains, volcanoes, jungle or marsh unless
following a road. They need $LINK<iron=GOOD_Iron> and $LINK<saltpeter=GOOD_Saltpeter>.

#DESC_PRTO_Cannon
^Gunpowder weapons came from China, and cannon appeared in Europe in the early fourteenth century. Within a
hundred years great bombards could bring down castle walls in days.
^
^The Ottoman cannon that breached the walls of Constantinople in 1453 showed that the age of the castle was over,
and fortress builders turned to low, thick walls that could withstand gunfire.

#PRTO_Artillery
^[Artillery] can bombard targets two squares away, and is powerful enough to wear down strong defenders. It
cannot attack or defend on its own, so it needs an escort.

#DESC_PRTO_Artillery
^In the late nineteenth century, breech-loading, rifled guns with recoil systems made artillery faster-firing,
more accurate and longer-ranged. Guns could now hit targets they could not see, directed by observers.
^
^Artillery caused most of the casualties of the First World War, and remained the main killer on the
battlefields of the twentieth century.

#PRTO_Radar_Artillery
^[Radar Artillery] is the most powerful land artillery. It bombards targets two squares away, moves two squares a
turn, has radar that lets it see two squares around it, and has a $LINK<zone of control=GCON_ZOC>. It cannot
attack or defend on its own. It needs $LINK<aluminum=GOOD_Aluminum>.

#DESC_PRTO_Radar_Artillery
^Modern self-propelled guns and rocket launchers are mounted on armored vehicles and directed by computers,
counter-battery radar and drones. They can fire within minutes of stopping and move off before the enemy
replies.
^
^Guided shells and rockets let a single gun hit a target tens of kilometers away with great accuracy.

#PRTO_Cruise_Missile
^A [Cruise Missile] is a one-shot weapon that flies to a target within range and bombards it, and is used up in
the attack. Its bombardment is lethal: it can destroy units on land and at sea. It needs
$LINK<aluminum=GOOD_Aluminum>.

#DESC_PRTO_Cruise_Missile
^A cruise missile is an unmanned, jet-powered flying bomb that guides itself to its target. The German V-1 of
the Second World War was an early, crude example.
^
^Modern cruise missiles fly low to avoid radar and use satellite navigation and terrain maps to strike with
great precision from hundreds of kilometers away.

#PRTO_NUKE
^A [Tactical Nuke] is a nuclear missile with a range of six squares. It destroys nearly everything where it lands,
wipes out much of a city's population, and leaves fallout behind. It can be carried by a
$LINK<Nuclear Submarine=PRTO_Nuclear_Submarine>. No one can build nuclear weapons until
$LINK<The Manhattan Project=BLDG_Manhattan_Project> has been built, and using one turns world opinion against
you. It needs $LINK<aluminum=GOOD_Aluminum> and $LINK<uranium=GOOD_Uranium>.

#DESC_PRTO_NUKE
^Atomic bombs were first built by the United States during the Second World War, and two were dropped on
Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. Later, smaller warheads were designed to be fired by missiles,
artillery and aircraft against targets on a battlefield.
^
^The terrible effects of these weapons, and the fear of escalation to all-out nuclear war, have so far kept them
from being used again.

#PRTO_ICBM
^An [ICBM] is a nuclear missile that can strike any square in the world from the city where it is based. It
destroys nearly everything at its target and leaves fallout behind, and using one turns world opinion against
you. No one can build nuclear weapons until $LINK<The Manhattan Project=BLDG_Manhattan_Project> has been built.
The $LINK<Strategic Missile Defense=BLDG_SDI> can shoot ICBMs down. It needs $LINK<aluminum=GOOD_Aluminum> and
$LINK<uranium=GOOD_Uranium>.

#DESC_PRTO_ICBM
^The intercontinental ballistic missile was developed by the Soviet Union and the United States in the late
1950s. Launched by rocket into space, its warheads fall on targets thousands of kilometers away within half an
hour.
^
^For decades both superpowers kept thousands of these missiles ready to fire. The certainty that any attack would
bring destruction in return became known as mutually assured destruction.

#PRTO_Curragh
^The [Curragh] is a small boat that can carry no units but lets a civilization explore its coasts early. It
must stay in coast squares: if it ends a turn in sea or ocean, it may sink.

#DESC_PRTO_Curragh
^The curragh is an Irish boat made of hides stretched over a wooden frame. Light and seaworthy, curraghs were
used for fishing, trade and travel along the coasts of Ireland and Scotland for thousands of years.
^
^Irish monks are said to have sailed curraghs as far as Iceland, and the legend of Saint Brendan tells of a
voyage across the Atlantic.

#PRTO_Galley
^The [Galley] is an early ship that can carry two units along the coast. It must stay in coast squares: if it ends
a turn in sea or ocean, it may sink. $LINK<The Great Lighthouse=BLDG_Lighthouse> makes galleys safe at sea.

#DESC_PRTO_Galley
^Galleys were long, narrow ships rowed by banks of oarsmen, with a sail for favorable winds. They dominated the
Mediterranean from ancient times until the seventeenth century.
^
^Warships like the Greek trireme rammed their enemies with a bronze beak, while merchant galleys carried cargo
and passengers from port to port, rarely leaving sight of land.

#PRTO_Caravel
^The [Caravel] is a sailing ship that can carry three units. It is safe in coast and sea squares, but may sink if
it ends a turn in the ocean.

#DESC_PRTO_Caravel
^The caravel was a small, nimble ship developed by the Portuguese in the fifteenth century. Its triangular
lateen sails let it sail close to the wind, which made it ideal for exploring unknown coasts.
^
^Caravels carried Portuguese explorers down the coast of Africa, and two of the three ships of Columbus's first
voyage in 1492 were caravels.

#PRTO_Galleon
^The [Galleon] is a large sailing ship that can carry four units and cross any water safely. It has little
fighting power.

#DESC_PRTO_Galleon
^Galleons were large, multi-decked sailing ships of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, used both as
warships and as cargo carriers.
^
^Spain's treasure fleets carried silver from the Americas home in galleons, and Manila galleons crossed the
Pacific to trade with Asia. Heavily armed, they could defend themselves against most pirates.

#PRTO_Frigate

#DESC_PRTO_Frigate
^Frigates were fast, three-masted warships with a single deck of guns. Smaller than ships of the line, they
served as scouts for battle fleets, escorted convoys and hunted enemy merchant ships.
^
^Their speed and independence made frigate command a prized post in the navies of the eighteenth and early

#PRTO_Privateer

#DESC_PRTO_Privateer

^
^English privateers such as Francis Drake raided Spanish shipping in the sixteenth century, and privateers

#PRTO_Ironclad

#DESC_PRTO_Ironclad

#PRTO_Transport
