# 2. City economy

How a Civ3 city turns tiles into food, shields and commerce, and what eats them. Scope is the base game, PTW and Conquests at their final patches (1.29f / PTW 1.27f / C3C 1.22). Scenarios, mods and the special Conquests game modes are out of scope.

## 1. Tile yields

- **Base terrain yields (C3C defaults).** Each worked tile gives its terrain's food/shields/commerce plus improvement bonuses. BIQ values:

  | Terrain | Food | Shields | Commerce | Irrigate | Mine | Road | Cities allowed |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Grassland | 2 | 0 | 0 | +1 | +1 | +1 | yes |
  | Plains | 1 | 1 | 0 | +1 | +1 | +1 | yes |
  | Desert | 0 | 1 | 0 | +1 | +1 | +1 | yes |
  | Flood Plain | 3 | 0 | 0 | +1 | 0 | +1 | yes |
  | Tundra | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | +1 | +1 | yes |
  | Hills | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | +2 | +1 | yes |
  | Mountains | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | +2 | +1 | no |
  | Forest | 1 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | +1 | yes |
  | Jungle | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | +1 | yes |
  | Marsh | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | +1 | no |
  | Volcano | 0 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | no |
  | Coast | 1 | 0 | 2 | - | - | - | no |
  | Sea | 1 | 0 | 1 | - | - | - | no |
  | Ocean | 0 | 0 | 0 | - | - | - | no |

  Source: [BIQ] TERR records. The Civilopedia tables agree on the land values, e.g. [PEDIA] `#GCON_Food`: "Floodplains 3 4 if irrigated. ... Hills 1 cannot be irrigated." and `#TFRM_Mine`: "Hills 1 shields, 3 if mined. ^Mountains 1 shields, 3 if mined." The water values are under UNVERIFIED (open question 1).
- **Bonus grassland** (grassland "with shield") gets +1 shield. Source: [PEDIA] `#TERR_Grassland_with_Shield`: "These have a rock outcropping to symbolize this and produce a $LINK<shield=GCON_Shields> if worked in a $LINK<city radius=GCON_Radius>." [PEDIA] `#TFRM_Mine`: "w/ outcropping 1 shields, 2 if mined."
- **Rivers** add +1 commerce to every tile they border and supply fresh water for irrigation. Source: [PEDIA] `#TERR_River`: "Any square with a river along its edge produces one extra $LINK<commerce=GCON_Commerce> if worked by citizen laborers within a $LINK<city radius=GCON_Radius>."
- **Irrigation** needs a river, a lake or an irrigated tile next to it, until Electricity. Source: [PEDIA] `#TFRM_Irrigation`: "Only squares adjacent to fresh water (river or lake) or to another irrigated square may be irrigated. After the discovery of $LINK<Electricity=TECH_Electricity>, you can irrigate a tile without proximity to fresh water."
- **Roads** give +1 commerce on all the land terrains listed (per-terrain `RoadBonus`). Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Commerce` table, e.g. "Grasslands 0 1 with road. ... Forests 0 1 with road." [BIQ] RoadBonus=1 for every land terrain except Volcano.
- **Railroads** add +1 food on an irrigated tile and +1 shield on a mined tile. They add nothing on an unimproved or forest tile. The Firaxis texts give no amount, so the numbers come from community tests (section 7 §4.3 has the full set). Sources:
  - [PEDIA] `#TFRM_Railroad`: "Railroads also increase the output of $LINK<irrigation=TFRM_Irrigation> or $LINK<mines=TFRM_Mine>."
  - Yeti, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/does-railroad-provide-extra-shield-on-mined-grassland-plain.74236/ (C3C era): "If there's a mine, RR adds 1 shield." and "If there's irrigation, RR adds 1 food."
  - David Weldon, https://apolyton.net/forum/miscellaneous/archives/civ3-strategy-archive/42626-distilled-civ3-tips-and-notes?p=953406 (vanilla): "RR through forest does nothing. RR through unimproved squares? Nothing."
- **Bonus resources** on a tile add (food/shields/commerce). From [BIQ] GOOD records (type 0 = bonus, 1 = luxury, 2 = strategic):

  | Resource | F/S/C | Resource | F/S/C | Resource | F/S/C |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Cattle (0) | 2/1/0 | Wheat (0) | 2/0/0 | Game (0) | 2/0/0 |
  | Fish (0) | 2/0/1 | Whales (0) | 1/1/2 | Oasis (0) | 2/0/0 |
  | Gold (0) | 0/0/4 | Sugar (0) | 1/0/1 | Tobacco (0) | 0/0/1 |
  | Tropical Fruit (0) | 1/0/1 | Gems (1) | 0/0/4 | Silks (1) | 0/0/3 |
  | Ivory (1) | 0/0/2 | Incense (1) | 0/0/2 | Spices (1) | 0/0/2 |
  | Furs (1) | 0/1/1 | Dyes (1) | 0/0/1 | Wines (1) | 1/0/1 |
  | Horses (2) | 0/0/1 | Iron (2) | 0/1/0 | Saltpeter (2) | 0/0/1 |
  | Coal (2) | 0/2/1 | Oil (2) | 0/1/2 | Rubber (2) | 0/0/2 |
  | Aluminum (2) | 0/2/0 | Uranium (2) | 0/2/3 | | |

  Source: [BIQ] GOOD. Patch history: [RM129] 1.17f: "Game bonus resource now gives +2 food to its tile."
- **Harbor**: +1 food on Coast/Sea/Ocean tiles. **Offshore Platform**: +1 shield on them. **Commercial Dock** (C3C): +1 commerce on water tiles that already make 1. Source: [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Harbor`: "Coastal, sea, and ocean squares produce one extra $LINK<food=GCON_Food>." `#BLDG_Offshore_Platform`: "causes all worked coastal, sea, and ocean squares to produce one $LINK<shield=GCON_Shields>." `#BLDG_Commercial_Dock`: "produce one extra Commerce in every water square that already produces one."
- **Colossus**: +1 commerce in its city's tiles that already make at least 1. Source: [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Colossus`: "causes its city to produce one extra $LINK<Commerce=GCON_Commerce> in every square that already produces at least one."
- **Lakes**: [PEDIA] says a fresh-water lake gives 2 food ("Fresh Water Lake 2 cannot be irrigated."). [RMPTW] says lakes are not their own terrain type: "Fresh water lakes are not a terrain type. They are composed of coastal tiles surrounded by land." So a lake tile is a Coast tile flagged as fresh water. Its food is under UNVERIFIED (open question 1).
- **City radius**: tiles within 2 squares of the city that are also inside its cultural borders. Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Radius`: "within the city's $LINK<cultural borders=GCON_Territory>, and ^* within two squares of the city."

### Government tile modifiers

- **Despotism (and Anarchy) tile penalty**: any tile yield above 2 is cut by 1. Both governments carry the BIQ flag. Sources:
  - [PEDIA] `#GOVT_Despotism`: "any city production square which produces more than two $LINK<food=GCON_Food>, $LINK<shields=GCON_Shields>, or $LINK<commerce=GCON_Commerce> in a despotic government instead produces one less."
  - CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "In Despotism, the standard government, all tiles that produce 3 or more food/shields/commerce will produce one less." Also: "That's why grassland (2 food), when irrigated (+1 food) in Despotism, will only produce 2 food (2 + 1 – 1 = 2)."
  - [BIQ] GOVT `TilePenalty=1` for both Anarchy and Despotism, 0 for all others.
  - The editor's wording differs (see Disputed): [EDHELP] "Flags (Government Properties)": "Standard tile penalty Under the selected government, no tile may generate more than 2 food and shields."
- **The penalty still applies during a Golden Age.** Source: CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "The bonuses from a Golden Age, however, are still subject to the Despotism tile penalty."
- **Republic and Democracy trade bonus**: +1 commerce on tiles. Sources:
  - [BIQ] `TradeBonus=1` for Republic and Democracy only.
  - CFC Civilopedia mirror, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/civilopedia/governments/ (Republic, Democracy): "Adds 1 commerce to any tile already producing at least 1".
  - [EDHELP] "Flags (Government Properties)": "Standard trade bonus Under the selected government, each tile generates an additional +1 to commerce." This wording omits the "already producing 1" condition (see Disputed).

### City center tile

- **Civ traits change the center tile.** Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Strengths`:
  - Agricultural: "the center city square and irrigated deserts produce one more food."
  - Commercial: "The center city squares of all cities and metros produce extra $LINK<commerce=GCON_Commerce>, and less $LINK<corruption=GCON_Corruption> is experienced."
  - Industrious: "the center city square of all cities produces extra $LINK<shields=GCON_Shields> in cities and metros."
  - Seafaring: "Each city built along the coast receives a commerce bonus in the center city square".

  C3C Agricultural detail, from https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ3-conquests-additions-changes-list.104294/ (Arathorn): "Provides one bonus food in the city square in all governments except despotism." and "In despotism, the bonus food only appears if the city is by fresh water."
- **A city tile normally gets a road.** It gets a railroad when the owner has Steam Power and the city is captured. Sources:
  - [RM129] 1.29f: "Changed city code so that a road/railroad is not placed under a city if a road/railroad is not allowed on the terrain type the city is built upon."
  - [RMC3C] 1.20: "Railroads will always appear under captured cities if you have Steam Power."
- **The city tile is always worked.** [MANUAL] p.75: "The terrain a city occupies is especially important, because it is always being worked. You cannot take the workforce off this square".
- **City-tile yield formula (community-tested; no Firaxis text).** The city tile does not get "free irrigation" as in Civ2. It has a fixed food value and size-based shield and commerce bonuses. Sources disagree on details by version, so each claim is marked:
  - **Food is 2, whatever the terrain** (3 for Agricultural, see the trait note above). Food bonuses on the tile are lost.
    - tjs282, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/city-tile-yield.665936/ (C3C, 2020): "a town-tile will only ever yield 2 food (or 3 if Agricultural and on freshwater/ out of Despotism)". justanick, same thread: "food production of the city tile does not depend on the tile".
    - German CivWiki, https://www.civ-wiki.de/wiki/Stadtfeldertrag_(Civ3) (rev. 54333, 2023): "Stadtfelder erzeugen immer mindestens zwei Nahrungseinheiten" ("city tiles always produce at least two food"), and Agricultural gives three, reduced to two by the Despotism penalty unless the city is on fresh water or already size 7 or more.
    - Nero Would, https://codehappy.net/apolyton/threads/49358-1.htm (vanilla 1.21f, May 2002): "Food production is always 2 for city centre tiles".
    - Code reading: [C3X-INI] `city_center_food_yield` describes stock as "All CC tiles produce enough food to feed one citizen regardless of terrain or resources, as in the base game", independent of "the agricultural bonus (+1 food on city center), the despotism yield penalty". So the value is one citizen's food (the BIQ food per citizen, 2 by default), not a hard-coded 2. [C3X-INI] also confirms the Agricultural exception: "Under the standard game rules, the Despotism tile penalty does not apply to food yield from city tiles of Agricultural civs with fresh water."
  - **Shields: at least 1, plus size bonuses.**
    - DaveMcW, https://codehappy.net/apolyton/threads/78438-1.htm (PTW era, Feb 2003) lists "Shields from unimproved terrain", "+Shields from bonus resources", "+1 if city size >= 7", "+1 if industrious and size >= 7", "+1 if city size >= 13", "+1 if shields==0 so far" and "+1 if in golden age/mobilization". He adds: "you only get the 0-shield-terrain bonus if the city is size 6 or less."
    - Nor Me and Thoth, same thread: the Industrious bonus comes only at size 13 or more in PTW ("I only get this in size >= 13. That's in PTW 1.14f." / "The Industrious bonus was pushed back to Metros in PTW.").
    - Bamspeedy's C3C "not bugs" list (https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/compiled-list-of-bugs-and-non-bugs-not-a-dicussion-thread.70063/, #33): "All civs get 1 extra shield in cities and metropolises (industrious gets 1 more than other civs in metropolises)." He lists the Civilopedia's "industrious civs get bonus shields in cities and metropolises" as a minor text bug.
    - German CivWiki (above): "+ 1 für Städte < 7, falls Grundertrag = 0", "+ 1 für Städte ≥ 7 und < 13", "+ 2 für Städte ≥ 13", with Industrious +1 more at 13 or more.
    - justanick, CFC 665936: "zero is sort of rounded up to 1 to prevent the possibility of zero production."
  - **Commerce.** The German CivWiki gives, for an ordinary city, "1 Handel, Stadtgröße < 7", "2 Handel, Stadtgröße ≥ 7 und < 13" and "3 Handel, Stadtgröße ≥ 13". Commercial adds "+2 Handel" at 7-12 and "+3 Handel" at 13 or more. Republic/Democracy, a river, the Colossus and a coastal Seafaring city add +1 each, and resources add 0-4. The capital's base is "4 Handel" whatever its size, and the higher of the two calculations is used. The page's own maximum ("4 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 4 = 14") does not match its table, so treat the Commercial figures with care. Section 8 (traits) has the CFC FAQ wording: Commercial city centres get extra commerce at City size and again at Metropolis size. Nero Would (codehappy 49358, 1.21f) saw a different rule then, "If the resulting Gold is less than 4, increase to 4", which he called "new in 1.21f". That is probably the capital rule.
  - **Despotism.** The −1 penalty on tiles above 2 applies to the city tile too. Nero Would (1.21f): "Under Despotism, production would be reduced to 2/2/3".
  - Status: consistent community findings across vanilla, PTW and C3C, with the Industrious bonus moving from size 7 (vanilla) to 13 (PTW/C3C). No Firaxis statement was found. Thread CFC 535361, cited in earlier drafts, is in the Civ2 forum and is not used.
- **Founding on forest or jungle clears the tile to its base terrain.** Section 7 §9 quotes piratebrun (Apolyton 85881): it "is converted to its base terrain." Nero Would (codehappy 49358): "In the case of Forest, use the production of the underlying terrain (Grassland, Plains or Tundra)." Chicken Pizza (CFC 665936) notes a forest "gets auto-cut and the grass under happens to be bonus." So inca911's "cleared to grassland" (https://apolyton.net/forum/miscellaneous/archives/civ3-strategy-archive/42626-distilled-civ3-tips-and-notes) holds only where the underlying terrain is grassland (see Disputed 10).
- **Settling on a food bonus loses it.** Source: CFC City Placement (C3C), https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/empire-management/city-placement/: "If you settle on food bonuses, you lose the food bonus."
  - Shield and commerce bonuses are kept (community). Alekseyev_ ([C3X-THREAD] post-16337774, page-51 #1010): "settling on food resources deletes all the extra food, while settling on prod or money resources does not cause such a loss." Virote_Considon (post-16985727, page-190 #3788) asks for cities to get (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "the food bonuses of resources on tiles they settle, as is already the case for shield and commerce bonuses". This fits the fixed one-citizen food value above.
  - Despotism penalty exception on the city tile (community, confirmed in code by Flintlock). tjs282 and justanick (post-16336379 and post-16336821, page-51 #1005/#1008) give two exceptions: the town has fresh water, or it has an Aqueduct and has reached city size. Flintlock (post-16337576, #1009): "that exception to the tile penalty is clearly apparent" in the code. He adds (post-16695556, page-118 #2360) that "the penalty is applied on a per-tile basis".

## 2. Food, growth and city size

- **Each citizen eats 2 food per turn.** Source: [BIQ] `FoodConsumptionPerCitizen=2`. [PEDIA] `#GCON_Food`: "[Every citizen must eat two food per turn or die.]"
- **Growth**: surplus food fills the food box. When the box is full it empties and the city gains 1 citizen. Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Food`: "When it is full, the storage is emptied and the city grows, adding one citizen to its population."
- **Food box sizes: 20 for sizes 1-6 (town), 40 for 7-12 (city), 60 for 13+ (metropolis).** No editor field exists for these, so they appear to be hard-coded.
  - Realms Beyond micromanagement guide (1.17f), http://www.realmsbeyond.net/civ/mmguide.html: "At and below six food boxes are 20 squares, while they're 40 from size 7-12."
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/granaries-dont-work.53078/ (MadScot, 1.29f): "A size 5 city needs 20 food to grow" and a size 7 city "needs a full 40 to gro to size 8".
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/pottery-is-underrated.683382/ (Spoonwood): "But, the box changes from 40 total food to 60 total food iirc." and "that test confirms that changing from size 12 to 13 keeps 20 food in the box".
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/is-there-a-way-to-manipulate-the-number-of-accumulated-food-required-for-city-growth.338390/ (Czulu): "However i cant find that option in civilization 3 conquests editor."
- **AI cities' boxes are scaled by the difficulty cost factor.** The human factor is 10. AI factors are Chieftain 20, Warlord 12, Regent 10, Monarch 9, Emperor 8, Demigod 7, Deity 6, Sid 4 ([BIQ] DIFF CostFactor). Source: [EDHELP] "Cost Factor (AI Bonuses)": "Determines the cost factor that is applied to AI growth, shields, and research. The player's cost factor is always 10."
- **Granary**: when the city grows, only half the box is used up, so it keeps half its food. The Pyramids give a Granary in every city on the continent (C3C). Sources:
  - [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Granary`: "effectively doubles the rate of a city's growth by causing only half of a city's $LINK<food store=GCON_Food> to be depleted when the city grows."
  - [BIQ] Granary flag `DoublesCityGrowthRate`.
  - [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Pyramids`: "Puts a $LINK<Granary=BLDG_Granary> in every city on the same continent."
  - https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/empire-management/the-case-for-food-or-how-to-become-economically-dominant/: "A town size 6 or below has a food box of 10 when the Granary is full."
- **Granary at the 6→7 and 12→13 steps:** disputed (see Disputed). The best-tested statement is that the granary keeps half of the *previous* box (10 food at 6→7, 20 food at 12→13) inside a box that has grown larger. Source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/pottery-is-underrated.683382/ (Spoonwood): "The granary keeps half of the food in the box that it had at the previous size." Patch history: [RM129] 1.17f: "Fixed infinite city growth exploit (involving granaries and size 6 cities)."
- **The granary only keeps food above the half-way mark.** It does nothing until the box is at least half full, and the half-way "line" appears only then. Source: Bamspeedy's C3C "not bugs" list, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/compiled-list-of-bugs-and-non-bugs-not-a-dicussion-thread.70063/ (#43): "When you get a granary (or pyramids), the food box must be at least half full before the 'line' appears" "(showing you that the food required is halved)."
- **The displayed "population" is cosmetic.** Theoden (C3C), https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/how-the-population-system-works.118422/: "A city has a base population of 0 at the size of 0" and "A city gains 1000 population for each food in the food box." His own test shows it has no rules effect, since the UN and domination count citizens: "counts citizens, not population."
- **Starvation**: if production plus stored food cannot feed everyone, one citizen starves. The population number shows red while food is negative. Sources:
  - [PEDIA] `#GCON_Food`: "If a city cannot feed its population either from new production or stored food, one of its citizens {starves} and disappears."
  - CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "if it is red, the city is losing food and citizens are starving."
  - Resisters do not eat. [RM129] 1.21f: "Corrected Resistance Civilopedia chapter to reflect resisting citizens do not consume food."
- **Civil disorder** stops food storage, production and revenue. Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Disorder`: "^* $LINK<Production=GCON_Shields> ceases, ^* No revenue comes in from $LINK<commerce=GCON_Commerce>, and ^* No excess $LINK<food=GCON_Food> is stored."
- **Size limits**: a town (size 1-6) needs an Aqueduct to pass 6, unless it is next to fresh water. A city (7-12) needs a Hospital to pass 12, and the Hospital needs size 7 or more. Shakespeare's Theatre also has the "allows level 3" flag. No cap is listed for metropolises. Sources:
  - [BIQ] RULE `MaximumLevel1CitySize=6`, `MaximumLevel2CitySize=12`, level names Town/City/Metropolis; Aqueduct `AllowsCitySize2`; Hospital and Shakespeare's Theatre `AllowsCitySize3`.
  - [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Aqueduct`: "A city with an [Aqueduct] can grow beyond population six. ^Note that a city adjacent to fresh water never requires an aqueduct."
  - [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Hospital`: "A city with a [Hospital] can grow beyond population 12. You must have a population of 7 or higher to build a hospital."
  - CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "need a Hospital (available with Sanitation) to grow past size 12."
- **No food carryover** beyond the box. Source: CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "No – there is no shield or food carryover for cities, just like there is no beaker carryover when researching a technology."

## 3. Shields, production and switching

- **Shield cost** = BIQ cost × cost factor (human 10, AI per difficulty). Source: [EDHELP] "Cost (Improvement Properties)": "Note that this value is multiplied by a cost factor which determines how many shields it actually costs to build the selected improvement/wonder. The human players cost factor is always 10."
- **Production multipliers are a percentage of base shields and add together.** BIQ `Production` is in 25% steps: Factory 2 (+50%), Manufacturing Plant 2, Coal/Hydro/Solar 2, Nuclear 4 (+100%), Iron Works 4. One power plant per city. Sources:
  - [EDHELP] "Production (Improvement Properties)": "in multiples of 25% (so if the value is set at 2, the building gives +50% total shield production)."
  - [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Factory`: "increases $LINK<shield production=GCON_Shields> in its city by 50%."
  - https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/game-mechanics/everything-you-wanted-to-know-about-power-plants/ (PTW 1.21f): Nuclear "adds a 100% production bonus to base shields". Its example: 32 base + 16 (factory) + 16 (plant) + 16 (Manufacturing Plant) = 80. Hoover Dam gives a Hydro Plant to every city on the continent ([PEDIA] `#BLDG_Hoover_Dam`).
  - **Tested in C3C 1.22:** a power plant adds 50% of base shields, not 50% of the Factory's bonus, so the Civilopedia's plant wording is stale. Catt, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/power-plants-everything-you-wanted-to-know-but-were-afraid-to-ask.52745/page-2 (Apr 2004): "the test was C3C v1.22, on a capital city - no waste - producing 49 base shields"; "with factory, nuclear plant, and mfg plant, the production was 147 shields"; "a power plant increases a city's base production by 50%, not only the _factory's_ production bonus". Also: "The civilopedia entries for the power plants still read: "The ____ plant increases factory output by 50%.""
  - Each city gets one power plant, and Hoover Dam does not stack with one. Catt (same article): "A city may have only one power plant." A plant keeps working after its resource is lost.
- **Waste** comes off shields first (see section 5). Multipliers apply only to the shields left after waste. Catt's example (power-plant article): a city working tiles "producing 38 shields, 6 of which are lost to waste, leaving 32 shields available", and "the citys factory adds 50% of 32, or 16 shields." alexman, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/do-you-think-you-understand-corruption-pre-c3c.19922/page-2 (#39): "their bonus is applied to the uncorrupted part of the city's production or commerce".
- **No shield overflow.** Surplus shields beyond the item's cost are lost. Sources:
  - CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "If you have extra shields being produced then are needed, they'll just go to waste."
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/short-rushing.119305 (SJ Frank): "then you end up wasting 20 shields of production on the next turn."
- **Wealth** turns shields into gold. The rate is a version difference: **8 shields per gold in vanilla and PTW, 4 in C3C**, and Economics halves it in each. Sources:
  - [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Wealth` (C3C): "[Wealth] causes shields to be converted to gold at a ratio of four to one. ^When your civilization learns $LINK<Economics=TECH_Economics> the ratio is reduced to two to one."
  - [PEDIA-V] and [PEDIA-PTW] `#BLDG_Wealth`: "[Wealth] causes shields to be converted to gold at a ratio of  eight to one. ^When your civilization learns $LINK<Economics=TECH_Economics> the ratio is reduced to four to one."
  - [BIQ] `ShieldsCostPerGold=4`; [BIQ-V] and [BIQ-PTW] RULE `ShieldsCostPerGold=8`. Wealth has the `Capitalization` flag. [EDHELP] "Shield Cost Per Gold": "Default is 8 gold." This is the base-game default left unchanged in the C3C help.
  - Vanilla, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/wealth.24405 (CamDawg, Jun 2002): "I think the default rate is 8 shields for 1 gold." and "Economics will cut this in half to 4 shields to 1 gold."
  - Rounding (vanilla, observed): etj4Eagle, same thread: "if you produce from 1-7 shields you will get 1 gold. If you produce 8-15 shields you get 2." This reads as at least 1 gold, then one more per full 8 shields. It is untested beyond that post.
  - [MANUAL] p.61 calls Wealth "essentially the same effect as Capitalization", but "the income it generates is greatly reduced".
- **Mobilization** (Nationalism) gives +1 shield on tiles making at least 1 while building military units, and only military units and improvements can be built. Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Mobilization`: "During mobilization a city building a military unit produces one extra $LINK<shield=GCON_Shields> in every square that is already producing at least one."
  - "Military unit" means attack above zero (code reading). Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16215014, page-29 #580), from the code: "And the reason bombers and artillery don't get the bonus while stealth fighters do is due to attack strength." (the unit must have attack above zero), and "So it's clear that the developers deliberately excluded troop transports from the mobilization bonus."
- **Clearing a forest** sends 10 shields to the nearest city's build, once per tile per game, and never to a wonder. Sources:
  - [BIQ] `ForestValueInShields=10`.
  - [RM129] 1.16f: "Shield bonus from clearing forest can only be received once per game."
  - CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "When you chop them down, 10 shields will be added to the current thing being built in the nearest city (provided it isn't a wonder)".
- **Civil Engineer** (C3C, Replaceable Parts) gives +2 shields, for improvements and wonders only. Sources:
  - [BIQ] CTZN `Construction=2`.
  - [EDHELP] "Construction Bonus (Citizen Properties)": "Applies only while building improvements and wonders (never to units)."
- **No penalty for switching production, between categories or within one.** The only loss is the overrun when the new item costs less than the shields already in the box. This matches the owner ruling already in the engine. Sources:
  - [MANUAL] p.141 ("Note for Experienced Players"): "There is no penalty for switching production in mid-stream, unless the new project costs fewer shields than are already accumulated, in which case you forfeit the excess shields as overrun."
  - [MANUAL] p.61 (changes from Civ2): "No penalty for changing projects: The penalty for changing production in mid-project is gone--except for any shields lost as overrun."
  - wilbill, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ-engineers.74944 (C3C, Jan 2004): "this abliity to switch projects in midstream has existed in Civ3 all along."
  - No Civ3 source claims a percentage penalty. The "50% penalty" claim in the inventory comes from a Civ4 thread (CFC 139564).
- **Civil Engineer shields are tracked separately (C3C).** Civil Engineer shields count only toward improvements and wonders. If you switch to a unit, they leave the box, and they come back if you switch back. Grille, CFC 74944 (#16, after testing): switching to artillery dropped the box to one shield ("CE shields removed"), and on switching back "I got the formerly contributed CE shields back." Also: "there's no abuse possible". If the city is building a unit while it has Civil Engineers, their shields are not stored: "Such shields would be lost."
- The other confirmed switching rules:
  - **Rushed or chopped shields cannot be switched into a wonder.** This is an intended rule, not a bug. Bamspeedy's C3C list, CFC 70063 (#41, "not bugs"): "If you have 'rushed' any shields (cash rush, pop rush, chopping forests)," "you can't used those shields to switch them to a wonder."

  - **Shields from disbanding cannot go into a wonder.** https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/disbanding-units-obtaining-extra-shields.118679 (Renata): "you won't be able to switch to a wonder until the current build completes."
  - **Unit-to-unit switches keep the shields.** https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/production-loss-when-switching-from-building-one-unit-to-another.137918/: "Its not lost, but saved until you switch back to the previous unit." Use with caution, because the thread's game version is unclear.
  - **C3C 1.22 rush-then-switch exploit with Civil Engineers.** https://apolyton.net/forum/civilization-series/civilization-iii/143965-v1-29-gold-rush-bug-exploit-spolier (pvzh): "It happens if civil engeneers were used, these extra shileds generated for every rush/switch cycle."
- **Disbanding a unit in a city** adds 1/4 of its shield cost, rounded down. This does not work for wonders, does nothing outside a city, and is not subject to waste. Sources:
  - CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "The number of shields added is 1/4 the shield cost of the unit, rounded down." and "If you disband a unit on any tile that is not a city, nothing happens: the unit just disappears."
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/disbanding-units-obtaining-extra-shields.118679 (Ginger_Ale): "none are lost due to corruption." (Zakharov): "all of the shields are added to the current build (except wonders)."
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/disbanding-question.18365 (BillChin): "I believe it is 25% of production cost."
  - Code reading: Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16881426, page-157 #3130): "disbanding only returns 25% of the shield cost".
- **Trait discounts lower the item's cost; they are not bonus shields (code reading).** Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16831749, page-140 #2788): the base game handles trait bonuses "to reduce the cost of a build instead of granting bonus shields", and (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "the function to determine the cost of something is given a building or unit ID and a player". (That fits §4: the gold price falls with the cost.)
- **Production can be changed at any time; an impossible wonder is switched off** (code reading). Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16693789, page-118 #2346): "In Civ 3 you can change production at any time"; (page-117 #2331, post-16686149): (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "the game will switch production off a wonder that can't be completed".

## 4. Hurrying production

- **The hurry method is set per government.** Paid labor (gold): Monarchy, Republic, Democracy. Forced labor (population): Despotism, Communism, Feudalism, Fascism. Anarchy has none. Sources:
  - [BIQ] GOVT `Hurrying` (0 none: Anarchy; 1 forced: Despotism, Communism, Fascism, Feudalism; 2 paid: Monarchy, Republic, Democracy).
  - [PEDIA] `#GCON_Hurry_Production`: "Under $LINK<Despotism=GOVT_Despotism>, $LINK<Communism=GOVT_Communism>, $LINK<Feudalism=GOVT_Feudalism>, or $LINK<Fascism=GOVT_Fascism> you hurry production in your cities by forced labor." Also: "Under $LINK<Monarchy=GOVT_Monarchy>, $LINK<Republic=GOVT_Republic>, and $LINK<Democracy=GOVT_Democracy> you can pay overtime and hire temporary workers to complete a project." Also: "You have [no] production if your civilization is in $LINK<Anarchy=GOVT_Anarchy>."
- **Gold cost = 4 gold × remaining shields, doubled if the box is empty.** Sources:
  - [BIQ] `ShieldValueInGold=4`.
  - [EDHELP] "Shield Value in Gold": "Determines how many gold pieces it costs to buy each shield when hurrying production of a city improvement. Default is 4 gold."
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/save-50-on-rush-buying.13252/ (tetley): "The price for hurrying is 4 gold/shield, if you already have a few shields produced. Otherwise it doubles."
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/whipping-to-rush-vs-rushing-with-gold.257616/ (swift_like_wind, C3C 1.22): "the shield value in gold is doubled from the editor setting if no work has been done on that build yet". Its example: Temple "240 to rush with no shields already there which is 8 gold per shield", "116 to rush, 4 gold per shield" with 1 shield in.
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/hurry-production.95782/ (ManOfMiracles): "4 gold per shield still needed".
  - [MANUAL] p.144 ("Rush Jobs"): "Rush jobs cost four times as much gold as the remaining shields needed for completion. (You cannot pay to rush a Wonder of the World.)"
- **Units and improvements use the same gold rate.** No source gives a separate unit rate or a non-linear term, and every unit example fits 4 gold per shield (8 from an empty box). The Civ2 formula (CFC 18881) and the Civ1 "ms×2 + ms²/20" formula do not apply to Civ3. Sources:
  - pol1, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/whipping-to-rush-vs-rushing-with-gold.257616/ (C3C): "if You rush w/o any shield You pay doubled price, so for worker You pay 10*8=80". Also: "You pay needed shields multiplied by 4".
  - WackenOpenAir, CFC 95782: "rush a spearmen for only 12 gold (3 missign shields x 4 gold per shield)". petermarkab, same thread: "to rush a marketplace (100 shields) from scratch would cost 800 gold" and "change production to a worker, rush that (costs 80 gold)".
  - The trait discounts lower the shield cost of an item, so the gold cost falls with it. ManOfMiracles (95782) warns that a calculator "doesn't account for reduced costs based on your civ traits". swift_like_wind's 30-shield Temple (CFC 257616) is a half-price Religious Temple: "240 to rush with no shields already there".
- **A rushed item appears at the start of your next turn.** [MANUAL] p.145: "Items completed by rush jobs are available at the beginning of your next turn, so there is no advantage to rushing items that would be complete on the next turn anyway."
- **No hurrying in a city with resisters or in disorder.** [MANUAL] p.149: "Note that you cannot rush completion of a job if there are any resistors in a city." [SCRIPT] `#HURRY_RESISTANCE` and `#HURRY_CIVIL_DISORDER` (section 5 §5.5).
- **The pop-rush rate is the BIQ "Citizen Value in Shields"** (code reading, Flintlock, [C3X-THREAD] post-16349903, page-53 #1058).
- **Population rush ("whip")**: each citizen is worth 20 shields. The number of citizens is ceil(remaining / 20), doubled if the box is empty. The rounding up matches the owner ruling in the engine. PaperBeetle (CFC 257616, p.2) gives the order: "the game first calculates that to whip 1<x<=20 shields "costs" 1 citizens", and the empty-box doubling comes after that ("if you whip a spear on an empty box, it takes 2 citizens"). You can never sacrifice more than half the city at once. Each sacrificed citizen causes 20 turns of unhappiness. Sources:
  - [BIQ] `CitizenValueInShields=20`, `TurnPenaltyForEachHurrySacrifice=20`.
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/how-is-pop-rush-done-now.128518/ (DBear, C3C 1.22): "Pop rush costs 1 population per 20 shields or fraction thereof." and "you cannot pop rush more than half your population at the same time."
  - https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/whipping-to-rush-vs-rushing-with-gold.257616/ (PaperBeetle): "when you whip on an empty box, the _cost_ is doubled, just as with cash rushing."
  - Patch history, [RM129] 1.21f: "The first citizen is now worth 20 shields instead of 40 shields, just like all the other citizens." and "Draft anger and hurrying unhappiness reduced to 20 turns." (1.17f had raised it to 40.)
- **Wonders cannot be hurried with gold or population.** Sources:
  - CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "you can't rush a wonder by sacrificing population (Despotism, Communism, Fascism), by rushing it with gold (other governments)".
  - Great Leaders differ by type (see Disputed). [PEDIA] `#GCON_Leaders`: a Military Leader can "$LINK<hurry=GCON_Hurry_Production> production of an Improvement or Small Wonder", and a Scientific Leader can hurry "any [improvement] (including a Great Wonder)".
  - C3C changes list, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ3-conquests-additions-changes-list.104294/: "Military Great Leaders (MGLs) can no longer rush Great Wonders." Same list: "An SGL can rush a wonder (either Great or Small)."
  - Before C3C, a leader could finish anything, wonders included. [MANUAL] p.145: "A leader, when entering a city, can complete whatever is under construction there." and "This is the only really effective way to rush the production of a Wonder of the World."
- **Draft** (Nationalism): cities of size 7 or more turn citizens into conscript defenders. The limit is 1 per turn in Republic/Democracy and 2 per turn in Despotism, Monarchy, Communism, Fascism and Feudalism. Anarchy cannot draft. Each draft causes 20 turns of unhappiness. Sources:
  - [PEDIA] `#GCON_Draft`: "A city in a $LINK<Democracy=GOVT_Democracy> or $LINK<Republic=GOVT_Republic> can draft [one] citizen per turn." Also: "You may only draft citizens from a city with ^a population of 7 or higher."
  - [BIQ] GOVT `DraftLimit` (Anarchy 0; Despotism, Monarchy, Communism, Fascism, Feudalism 2; Republic, Democracy 1); `TurnPenaltyForEachDraftedCitizen=20`.
  - https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/empire-management/how-to-pop-rush-in-republicdemocracy/: "1 citizen unhappy for 20 turns for each citizen drafted/whipped."
- **Partial ("short") rushing**: rush part of the cost by switching to a cheaper item, then switch back. Source: https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/short-rushing.119305 (WackenOpenAir): "short rushing is rushing part of an object by swiching to something else and then back."

## 5. Corruption and waste

Corruption takes commerce from worked land tiles. Waste takes shields. Both use the same model unless noted.

- **Causes**: distance from the capital, number of cities, and government. Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Corruption`: "In general, the farther a city is from its capital city, the more corruption and waste it experiences. Also the more cities in your empire, the higher rates of both, overall."
- **C3C formula** (alexman). Source: https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/game-mechanics/everything-about-corruption-c3c-edition/ : "The total corruption in each city is equal to the sum of two independent components: distance corruption, and rank corruption."
  - Distance to the nearest palace (Palace or FP, see Disputed): `d = max(x,y) + min(x,y)/2`, with integer division rounded down. x and y are the tile offsets along the map's two diagonal axes.
  - `MaxD = (MapW + MapH) / 4`.
  - Communal governments use `d = MaxD / 4` for every city.
  - `da = 0.5^Ni * min(Gd * t * d, MaxD)`, then `Cd = da / MaxD`.
  - `Nopt = max(OCN * (L/100 * (1 + c + Gr + Gp*Nwe) + 0.25*Ni), 1)`.
  - Rank R: "all cities of an empire are ranked in order of distance to the capital, starting at zero for the capital itself." Ties go "in order of founding" and then database order. Communal: "all cities have the same rank, which is half the total number of cities in the empire, rounded down."
  - `Cr = R / (2*Nopt)` if `R < Nopt`, else `(2*R - Nopt) / (2*Nopt)`.
  - Cap: `Cmax = max(0.9 - (0.1*Ni + 0.7*Nwc), 0)`. Total = min(Cd + Cr, Cmax). The article states the sum and the cap separately. alexman confirms that the cap applies to the total (CFC 76619 #61, below).
  - Corrupted commerce = total × raw commerce from worked land tiles, "rounded to the closest integer".
  - Factors:

    | Symbol | Meaning | Values |
    |---|---|---|
    | Gd | government distance factor | Rampant (Despotism) 3/2; Minimal (Democracy) 3/4; others 1 |
    | t | trade network | 1 if connected to the capital by road, harbor or airport; 5/4 if not |
    | Gr | government rank factor | Minimal or Nuisance 0.1; Communal 2; others 0 |
    | Gp | per-wonder OCN factor | non-communal 3/8; communal 3 |
    | c | Commercial trait | 0.25, else 0 |
    | Ni | Courthouse / Police Station in the city | each halves distance corruption, raises Nopt, lowers the cap by 0.1 |
    | Nwe | active corruption wonders in the empire (FP, SPHQ) | count |
    | Nwc | corruption wonder in this city | lowers the cap by 0.7 |
    | OCN | optimal cities for the map size | Tiny 14, Small 17, Standard 20, Large 28, Huge 36 ([BIQ] WSIZ) |
    | L | % of optimal cities for the difficulty | Chieftain 100, Warlord 95, Regent 90, Monarch 85, Emperor 80, Demigod 70, Deity 60, Sid 50 ([BIQ] DIFF) |
  - **The empire-wide OCN term in the 1.22 code** (C3X author's reading of the executable, https://github.com/maxpetul/C3X/blob/master/Notes/ocn_formula.txt): "ocn = max (1, floor(s_diff * (b + n_fp + n_com + n_gov + n_ai) / 100))", where b is the map's base OCN and s_diff the player's own "percentage of optimal cities". The terms:
    - "n_fp = floor((3*f*b) / d)", f = number of Forbidden Palaces, "d = 8 if L's govt does NOT have communal corruption else 1". This matches Gp = 3/8 and 3 above.
    - "n_com = floor(b/4) if L has the trait else 0". This matches c = 0.25.
    - "n_gov = | floor(b/8) if L's govt has minimal or nuisance corruption | floor(b/16) if problematic | 2*b if communal | 0 otherwise". This differs from alexman's Gr: 1/8 (not 0.1) for Minimal and Nuisance, and 1/16 (not 0) for Problematic (Monarchy, Feudalism). Communal 2 matches.
    - "n_ai" is an AI-only term tied to the game difficulty (Monarch b/8, Emperor b/4, Demigod and above b/2; see `08-ai-difficulty-and-victory.md` 1.2).
    - Each term is floored separately, and so is the product. The per-city courthouse and police station term (0.25·Ni) is not part of this function.
    The code reading postdates alexman's article and is the more exact source for these factors. On a Standard map (b = 20) the differences are small: Monarchy gets floor(20/16) = 1 extra optimal city, and Republic floor(20/8) = 2 rather than 0.1 × 20 = 2.
  - **Waste only**: during WLTKD, "divide da by 2" and "add OCN/4 to Nopt".
  - **Policemen** remove 1 corrupted commerce and 1 wasted shield each, before the cap is applied. Source: [EDHELP] "Corruption Bonus (Citizen Properties)": "this citizen makes 1 corrupted commerce and 1 corrupted shield become uncorrupted." [BIQ] Policeman `Corruption=1` (Nationalism).
- **Government corruption levels** ([BIQ] GOVT `Corruption`; [PEDIA] GOVT entries):

  | Government | Level | Hurry | Draft | Tile penalty | Trade bonus | Building maintenance |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Anarchy | Catastrophic | none | 0 | yes | no | none |
  | Despotism | Rampant | forced | 2 | yes | no | yes |
  | Monarchy | Problematic | gold | 2 | no | no | yes |
  | Feudalism | Problematic | forced | 2 | no | no | yes |
  | Communism | Communal | forced | 2 | no | no | yes |
  | Fascism | Nuisance | forced | 2 | no | no | yes |
  | Republic | Nuisance | gold | 1 | no | yes | yes |
  | Democracy | Minimal | gold | 1 | no | yes | yes |

  Source: [EDHELP] "Corruption and Waste (Government Properties)": "Minimal is the lowest possible value; communal is the highest."
- **The capital has zero corruption, fixed rather than computed.** [C3X-INI]: "Normally the corruption rate in the capital is fixed at zero." Underneath that, the capital also has "a strong inherent bonus reducing corruption": "This bonus is incorporated into the decorrupting effect from buildings in the city and each point is equivalent in strength to one courthouse. For standard game rules, set to 10." (The bonus matters only if the zero is removed.) Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16906674, page-167 #3321) says the same from the code: "There's a rule in the corruption calculation that capitals always have zero corruption." The rest of the formula is "computed by one large function in the game, about 350 lines decompiled" (post-16853988, page-148 #2959), and all of it is integer arithmetic (post-16902126, page-166 #3309). Source: [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Palace`: "It eliminates $LINK<corruption/waste=GCON_Corruption> in the capital, and decreases it in nearby cities." https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/everything-about-corruption-c3c-edition.76619/ (alexman): "The Palace always has zero corruption".
- **The difficulty corruption modifier does not apply in some cases.** It is 100% at every level by default, and it is skipped for catastrophic governments, cities in disorder and the capital. Source: [EDHELP] "Corruption Modifier (Difficulty Level)": "The Corruption Modifier has no effect on players whose government experiences catastrophic corruption, cities in civil disorder, or capital cities. In these situations, corruption is automatically set and no formula is calculated." [BIQ] DIFF `CorruptionPercentage=100` at every level. In Anarchy no taxes are collected ([PEDIA] `#GOVT_Anarchy`: "corruption and waste are so high that no production occurs and no taxes are collected").
- **Forbidden Palace**: a Small Wonder, one per civ. It needs OCN/2 cities and raises OCN (via Nwe). Sources:
  - [EDHELP] "Reduces Corruption (Small Wonder Flag)": "the selected Small Wonder acts as a second "capitol city" ... The number of cities a player must have to enable construction of any wonder with this flag is equal to the Optimal Number of Cities divided by 2."
  - [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Forbidden_Palace`: "Requires: The civilization must have at least ten cities (on a standard size map)".
  - [RM129] 1.17f: "Forbidden Palace cost decreased to 200."
  - [RMC3C] 1.20: "Corruption calculation error that resulted in negative effects from the Forbidden Palace is now correct."
- **Secret Police HQ** (C3C): Communism only. It works like the FP and stops working if you leave Communism. Source: [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Secret_Police_HQ`: "Only civilizations under Communist government can build the Secret Police HQ. Changing from Communism will cause the Secret Police HQ bonuses to stop." [RMC3C] 1.20: "Secret Police HQ no longer gives corruption OR OCN bonus when player leaves Communism."
- **Courthouse** (maintenance 1) and **Police Station** (maintenance 2): reduce corruption in their own city. Source: [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Courthouse`: "decreases $LINK<corruption=GCON_Corruption> in its city and makes the city more resistant to propaganda." Patch history:
  - [RM129] 1.16f: "Police Stations now reduce corruption in the city they are built in."
  - [RM129] 1.21f: "Increased corruption/waste fighting ability of courthouses and police stations."
  - [RM129] 1.16f: "Corruption calculations for Communism are now flat for the number-of-cities factor."
- **What C3C changed, per Firaxis.** Tavis (Firaxis), https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/firaxis-corruption-breakdown.73485/ (Dec 30 2003, C3C beta): the formula was changed only to allow several "Reduce Corruption" small wonders and the new corruption-fighting specialists. Otherwise the calculation is the same as Civ3/PTW, with no values changed apart from multiple Forbidden Palaces and city ranking. (Paraphrased: the fetch tool would not give the post verbatim.) The FP bug in the C3C release was that rank was measured from the real capital, through `vectorDist(pRealCapital->getX(), ...)`, rather than from the closest capital. Tavis (#15) agreed with anarres that distance corruption was right and rank was wrong: "distance corruption is working correctly and is the primary reason that the bug wasn't found right away". Tavis (page 8, Jan 2 2004): "The current plan is to return it to pre-C3C w/ the fixes outlined above." Players report the corrected model shipped in C3C 1.15 (Chieftess, page 14: "If you're using the 1.15 patch, then the FP only increases the OCN"). [RMC3C] 1.20 lists the final fix.
- **Firaxis' rank-corruption code (C3C beta).** Tavis posted the ranking section of the source, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/firaxis-corruption-breakdown.73485/page-7 #124 ("For the coders, here's the expanded section on what was posted earlier"). It confirms the community rank model:
  - Communal: `cityRank = leaders[getWho()].getCities() / 2;` (integer division, so rounded down).
  - Otherwise `cityRank` counts the civ's other cities that are nearer the reference capital (`vectorDist(pRealCapital->getX(), ...)` in the buggy beta), with ties broken by `getDateFounded()` and then by `getID()`.
  - `if (cityRank < optimalNumberOfCities)` `cityCorruption = ((shieldsOrTradeArrows * cityRank) + 1) / 2;` else `cityCorruption = ((shieldsOrTradeArrows * ((cityRank * 2) - optimalNumberOfCities)) + 1) / 2;`. This is the `R/(2*Nopt)` and `(2*R - Nopt)/(2*Nopt)` shape above. The excerpt does not show the division by the optimal number of cities, nor the scaling of `shieldsOrTradeArrows`, so it does not settle the final rounding (Disputed 13). The `+ 1) / 2` is an integer round-half-up of the halving step.
  - Tavis, same post: "The Forbidden Palace does not provide a new cityranking Corruption ring", and "changing it to pClosestCity doesn't affect anything".
- **PTW vs C3C, per alexman.** CFC 76619 p.4 (#74, Jul 2004): distance corruption is unchanged ("Yes, distance corruption is the same in both versions."). The differences are "the Forbidden Palace model", the "OCN bonus for communal corruption", and RCP, the "ring city placement" that the C3C rank tie-break no longer rewards. Dianthus (#76): "there's no point using RCP in C3C".

- **Vanilla/PTW corruption formula** (alexman, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/do-you-think-you-understand-corruption-pre-c3c.19922/, Apr 2002, later "updated for PTW"; the moderator retitled it "specific to Vanilla/PTW"). alexman (#20) says it "is not 100% accurate" but is never off "by more than a couple percent":
  - Total: "Corruption% = 100 * (Fd*Fg*Fi*Ft + Fc)", capped: "Corruption can never exceed 95%, no matter what."
  - Distance: "Distance = floor(max(x,y) + 0.5*min(x,y))", with x and y "the distance in the NW/SE and NE/SW respectively". The capital counts as distance 1: "It looks like the capital is taken as a distance 1 tile (not zero) from itself".
  - Fd: "3.6 times the distance, divided by the map size". For a non-square map, average the two sides: alexman (#33) said averaging was "in fact what I used for the corruption calculation program". Communism: "corruption does not depend on distance", and Fd = 1.0.
  - Fg (government): Despotism 1, Monarchy 2/3, Republic 2/3, Democracy 4/9, Communism 0.3.
  - Fi = 0.5^N, where N counts the city's courthouse and police station. WLTKD adds 1 to N for waste only.
  - Ft = 0.85 if the city is "connected to the capital through roads, harbors, or airports".
  - Number of cities: "Fn = d*(1 + g + fp + imp*N + c)/100". Here d is the difficulty's "percentage of optimal cities" and g is "0.1 if Republic or Democracy, 0.2 if in Communism, 0.0 otherwise". fp is "zero for no FP, otherwise 0.2 for Communism and 0.1 for other governments". imp is "0.1 for Communism, 0.25 otherwise". c is "0.25 for Commercial civs, 0.0 otherwise".
  - Ncity is the number of cities closer to the capital than this one, the capital included. Fc = Ncity/(2·Fn·Nopt) if Ncity ≤ Fn·Nopt, else Ncity/(Fn·Nopt) − 0.5. Communism uses "0.1 times the ratio of ALL cities to (Fn*Nopt)".
  - FP: distance is the minimum of the distances to the capital and the FP. With the Qitai fix, rank for cities nearer the FP "is given by the number of cities that are closer to the Capital".
  - Rounding: the percentage times the shields or commerce, rounded "to the nearest integer" (verified by DaviddesJ). The 2002 Apolyton Corruption FAQ says rounded down (see Disputed 13). DaviddesJ, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/do-you-think-you-understand-corruption-pre-c3c.19922/page-5 #85 (Jun 2003): "round to the nearest integer"; with 10% corruption, 5-14 shields lose 1. alexman repeats it at page 7 #134 (add up the tile output, multiply by the percentage, "round to the nearest integer").
  - Minimum output. alexman (page 7 #136): "you get at least 1 shield and 1 commerce no matter what's going on with corruption".
  - What is corrupted. alexman (#134): the government tile penalty, Golden Age and mobilization bonuses are corrupted, because they "change the number of shields/commerce you get from some land tiles". (#136, hedged "As far as I know"): "corrupted commerce does not change when you change the sliders".
  - Test of courthouses past 2×OCN. Doc Tsiolkovski (page 7 #139, unexplained): below 2×OCN a courthouse or police station each cut corruption by about 10%; above it, "WltKD has **zero** effects" while both buildings together had a "huge additional effect (~-30%!)". The post is from the C3C era (post-2766333) and names no version. If it is C3C, it fits the cap term (each building lowers Cmax by 0.1) once corruption sits at the cap.
  - An earlier 1.21f form of the city-count term, alexman, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/do-you-think-you-understand-corruption-pre-c3c.19922/page-3 #41 (May 2002): "Nopt = OptimalCities \* (1 + N/4) \* DifficultyPercent / 100 + Nciv", with "Nciv = 1 for commercial civs. Zero otherwise" and "Add one [to N] for waste calculations for a city in WLTK". Arathorn (#55) and alexman (#56) agree the courthouse, police station and WLTKD quarters add rather than multiply. The final post-1 formula above (c = 0.25) supersedes the "+ Nciv" form.
  - The capital and the FP city do not count toward the city-count ranking. alexman (#47): "the FP (and capital) city does not count against the optimal number of cities limit", so "having a FP actually reduces corruption a bit". With an FP every city is on both lists, ranked by "the minimum rank of that city out of the two lists" (alexman, page 4 #68).
  - Marketplaces and factories don't change the percentage. alexman (#47): "the corruption (and waste) percentage is not affected by marketplaces (and factories)".
  - Distance metric test. DaviddesJ (page 4 #78): "each orthogonal move costs 1.0 and each diagonal move costs 1.5", and "a test city at distance (4,3) has exactly the same corruption as a test city at distance (5,1)."
  - Patch history: alexman (#39) says 1.21f changed only "the effect of police stations, courthouses, and WLTK". [RM129] 1.16f: "Corruption effects have been reduced."
  - Firaxis comment, relayed: BillChin (19922 #36) quotes a Soren Johnson chat on courthouses and police stations: "they basically cut the difference in half and add 1/4 of the optimal # of cities" and "courthouses only effect the city they are in".
  - Bug: maps of custom size use the Standard OCN. alexman (#39): "the game uses the optimal number of cities for a standard-sized map, no matter what the size of your actual map".
  - PTW rank exploit (fixed by the C3C model). Qitai, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/a-rank-corruption-discovery-and-exploit-to-negate-rank-corruption.62851/ (Aug 2003, tested on 1.29f): rank counts only "Number of cities with distance FROM PALACE less than Fd". SirPleb (#5): "can confirm that the effect you've discovered still exists in PTW 1.27." alexman (#19): "for sure a bug".

## 6. Commerce, the trade network and the sliders

- **Commerce sources**: tiles (terrain, road, river, resources), the government trade bonus, Golden Age, Colossus, Commercial Dock, and trait center-tile bonuses (sections 1 and 9).
- **Order of operations**: corruption is removed from raw commerce. The rest is split by the sliders, then each share gets its own multipliers. Bonuses add together (a percentage of the base, not compounded) and are rounded down. Specialists are not multiplied. Source: https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/empire-management/multiplier-buildings-a-practical-primer/: "Raw commerce has corruption taken off the top, with the remainder being allocated to science and luxury spending." Also: "Note that the increase is not compound but simple as it adds to the base rate." Also: "specialist output is never affected by city improvements".
- **Multiplier buildings** (+50% each, [BIQ] flags): tax = Marketplace, Bank, Stock Exchange; science = Library, University, Research Lab. Marketplace also raises luxury output. Source: [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Marketplace`: "increases tax revenue allocated to the $LINK<treasury=GCON_Commerce> by 50%". The Marketplace's luxury-resource table is in [PEDIA] (1/2/4/6/9/12/16/20 happy faces for 1-8 luxuries).
- **Sliders move in 10% steps.** Source: https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/empire-management/effective-commericial-management/ ("intervals of 10").
- **Rate cap**: no default ruleset has a cap. Every government's `ScienceRateCap` is 10 (100%) in C3C, and also in [BIQ-V] and [BIQ-PTW] (all six governments, read at the C3C offset; the corruption, hurry and draft fields read at the same layout match the known values). [MANUAL] p.41 mentions only the overall limit: "You can't allot more than 100%--no deficit spending!" Source: [EDHELP] "Rate Cap (Government Properties)": "Determines the maximum science funding (0-10) that the selected government can engage in. Default is always 10 (100%)." [BIQ] all 8 GOVT `ScienceRateCap=10`.
- **Specialists**: no specialist output is reduced by corruption. Sources:
  - [BIQ] CTZN values: Entertainer 1 luxury, Tax Collector 2 gold, Scientist 3 beakers, Policeman 1 corruption, Civil Engineer 2 shields.
  - https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/empire-management/the-role-of-the-specialist-citizen/: in 1.29 and PTW "three tax collectors return three gold to the treasury" (so 1 each), and in Conquests "the scientist's beaker output rose to three". Also: "Corruption and waste do not reduce the specialist's output".
  - CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "The taxman provides 2 gold per turn (only 1 in Vanilla and Play the World)."
- **Commerce rounding detail (C3C).** Multipliers are summed, applied to the share, and then rounded down. PaperBeetle, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/multiplier-buildings-a-practical-primer.242296/: "(2) summation of mulitipliers, (3) application of multiplier to base, (4) rounding down." How the slider split itself is rounded is UNVERIFIED (no source found).
- **Banks work without a Marketplace** (tested, C3C 1.22). Catt, CFC 52745 p.2: "Banks continue to provide a 50% bonus to taxes even if the market is destroyed".
- **Government trade bonus and tourism are added before corruption.** These are player statements, not tests. MAS (CFC 242296): the Republic/Democracy +1 "comes even before corruption" and "The tourist output is commerce added to the commerce from the land, also before corruption." CKS, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/interturn-mechanics.443195/page-2: "Tourist income is, indeed, corrupted." The changes list (CFC 104294) disagrees: tourist attractions "produce revenue (uncorrupted, not affected by buildings) based on their age", and "It starts at 2 gpt after 1000 years and can grow as high as 14 gpt." (Disputed 14.)
- **Wall Street** pays 5% interest on the treasury, capped at 50 gold per turn. Source: [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Wall_Street`: "The $LINK<treasury=GCON_Treasury> begins earning 5% interest every turn with a maximum cap of 50 gold per turn." It needs 5 Stock Exchanges. Code reading: Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16881426, page-157 #3130): "Whenever the game needs to compute a player's gold income, it separately computes the interest then applies the limit." The 50 is a one-byte constant in the instruction (post-16779611, page-132 #2639).
- **Trade network / connected cities.** Connected cities share every resource. Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Trade`: "Two cities are connected if any one of the following are true: ^* there is a $LINK<road=GCON_Worker_Jobs> or $LINK<railroad=GCON_Worker_Jobs> running between them, ^* there are $LINK<harbors=BLDG_Harbor> in both cities and a visible water route between them, ^* or both cities contain an $LINK<airport=BLDG_Airport>." Conditions:
  - **Water routes** need the right sea tech for every tile crossed. [PEDIA] `#GCON_Trade`: "your civilization must be able to safely traverse ^every square of the route and this depends upon having $LINK<Map Making=TECH_Map_Making>, $LINK<Navigation=TECH_Navigation>, and/or $LINK<Magnetism=TECH_Magnetism>."
  - **Enemy territory and blockades break the link.** [PEDIA] `#GCON_Trade`: "two cities are not connected if the only road/rail that connects them passes through enemy territory, or if a harbor is blockaded by enemy naval forces."
  - **Foreign trade** needs both capitals connected. [PEDIA] `#GCON_Trade`: "Excess resources may be traded to other civilizations if the capital cities of both civilizations are connected."
  - **Colonies** outside your borders need a road to a city. [PEDIA] `#GCON_Colony`: "you need a road connecting the colony to one of your cities to get the benefits of the resource."
  - **Corruption**: being connected to the capital lowers distance corruption (factor t, section 5). Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Corruption`: "Being attached to your capital via road, harbor, or airport also reduces corruption and waste."
  - **How the network is built (code re-implementation).** Flintlock's "Trade Net X" (https://github.com/maxpetul/C3X/tree/master/Trade%20Net%20X, `TradeNetX.cpp`, on by default in C3X) "reimplements parts of the game's "Trade Net" module to improve performance", reusing pathfinder results; it is meant to give the stock results faster, so its rules describe stock 1.22. Road network: a flood fill over the 8 neighbours of the city ("The original code iterates over neighbors in this non-standard order. I've replicated that here"). A tile is part of a civ's network if it has a road and is unowned, owned by a civ it is not at war with, or the civ is the barbarians (`has_road_open_to`). Roads in the territory of a civ at war with you don't count, and nothing else does: rivers are not part of the road network. City and colony tiles count as road only if their owner knows the Build Road job's tech. Water routes (`can_trade_via_water_at`): every tile must have been explored by the civ; a land tile is passable only if it holds a city; Coast needs nothing; Sea needs a tech with the "Enables Trade Over Sea Tiles" flag *or* a wonder with "Safe Sea Travel" (the Great Lighthouse); Ocean needs a tech with "Enables Trade Over Ocean Tiles". A water tile in the territory of a civ you are at war with, or holding its combat unit, breaks the route. That is the blockade rule in [PEDIA].
- **No per-route trade commerce.** No source found describes Civ2-style trade-route income. The [PEDIA] `#GCON_Trade` entry covers only resource sharing and connection rules. Treat "trade routes" as connections only. The absence of route income is UNVERIFIED.
- **How connections are computed (code readings, [C3X-THREAD], Flintlock).** These agree with the Trade Net X bullet above.
  - Per civ: "The game evaluates city connections for each civ independently" (post-16327160, page-49 #961).
  - Air and sea (post-16792735, page-135 #2683): "all it does for air routes is mark each city as connected to all others with airports"; for sea routes "it also tries finding a path between each pair of cities with harbors". The sea pass covers "all pairs of cities with harbors that are not already connected by roads" (post-16536108, page-83 #1646). Roads are flood-filled outward from each city (#2683). A trade route does pass through a road junction (post-16220142, page-30 #592: "But first, trade routes do pass through the intersections as far as I can tell.").
  - Blockades: "Hostile naval units already block trade connections" (post-16843464, page-144 #2875), as [PEDIA] says.
  - Recomputation: the network is recomputed "whenever a road is destroyed" (post-16401798, page-64 #1270).
- **Resource access (code readings, [C3X-THREAD], Flintlock).**
  - Each road tile linked to a city belongs to a trade network, and strategic and luxury resources are added to their tile's network (post-16783087, page-133 #2653).
  - Bonus resources are not on the network at all: "They can't be traded and in fact aren't even propagated through trade networks." (post-16544398, page-87 #1732); "the game doesn't add bonus resources to the trade network" (post-16826088, page-137 #2730). They count only on their own tile.
  - At least one copy must be connected. tjs282 (post-16328138, page-49 #972): (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "at least one instance of a resource must be connected to your trade-net, for that resource to be usable." Vuldacon (post-16090229, page-11 #214): resources (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "do have to have a road for use in a City and still be part of the Tradenet."
  - One full set per player: "The game only stores one full set of resource info per player." / "This is why the phantom resource bug exists." (post-16581087, page-94 #1870). Cities off the capital's network keep their own 32-bit field, in which (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "resources #1 and #33 share bit #1" (post-16172297, page-23 #454). Standard C3C has 26 resources, so the bug does not arise in stock rules (section 7 §6.2). A display bug follows from the same storage: the city screen shows the capital network's copy counts for every city (post-16401798, quoted on page-65 #1297).
  - Order and staleness: "resources on tiles are all processed before the buildings" (post-16284834, page-38 #748), and, a stock bug, "the game doesn't recompute resource access when a building is lost unless the building was enabling air or sea trade" (post-16284562, page-38 #743).
- **Cached values and missed recomputations (stock bugs, code readings).** "yields in cities are cached, they are not recomputed each time they're needed" (post-16737424, page-127 #2521). Some building effects go unapplied: "certain improvement effects wouldn't be applied because they need to be manually recalculated" (post-16319341, page-46 #914), for example between turns or when the luxury slider is moved. The intended rule is that every value is current; the engine recomputes.
- **Luxury resources**: each connected luxury makes one citizen happy (Marketplace scales this). Source: CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "For each luxury resource connected to your cities, you'll make one citizen happy."

## 7. Building maintenance

- **Maintenance per turn** (C3C, [BIQ] BLDG `MaintenanceCost`):
  - 0: Palace, Walls, Coastal Fortress, Wealth, all wonders.
  - 1: Barracks, Granary, Temple, Marketplace, Library, Courthouse, Aqueduct, Bank, Harbor, Civil Defense.
  - 2: Cathedral, University, Colosseum, Recycling Center, Hospital, Research Lab, Mass Transit, SAM Battery, Airport, Police Station, Commercial Dock.
  - 3: Factory, Manufacturing Plant, Coal/Hydro/Nuclear/Solar Plant, Offshore Platform, Stock Exchange.

  The [PEDIA] `#GCON_Maintenance` lists match these. Patch history: [RM129] 1.17f "Coastal Fortress maintenance reduced to 0."; [RMPTW] 1.01f "The maintenance cost of the Stock Exchange is increased from 2 to 3."
- **No maintenance in Anarchy.** Source: [BIQ] Anarchy `RequiresMaintenance=0`. [PEDIA] `#GOVT_Anarchy`: "There is no improvement maintenance when a civilization is in Anarchy." [EDHELP] "Flags (Government Properties)": "Requires maintenance Under the selected government, all city improvements costs must be paid for".
- **Smith's Trading Company** pays upkeep for trade buildings. The list changed by version (see Disputed). Source: [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Trading_Company` (C3C): "Pays $LINK<maintenance=GCON_Maintenance> costs for all trade-based city improvements ($LINK<Harbors=BLDG_Harbor>, $LINK<Marketplaces=BLDG_Marketplace>, $LINK<Banks=BLDG_Bank>, $LINK<Airports=BLDG_Airport>, $LINK<Commerical Dock=BLDG_Commercial_Dock> and $LINK<Stock Exchanges=BLDG_Stock_Exchange>)."
- **Buildings granted by wonders** (e.g. the Pyramids' granaries) cost no upkeep. Source: [RM129] 1.21f: "Fixed bug involving maintenance costs and granaries/Pyramids, barracks/Art of War, etc." Code reading: Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16783087, page-133 #2653) says buildings granted by wonders are maintenance-free; Alekseyev_ (post-16310808, page-44 #874): maintenance costs "are waived for free buildings." (C3X's changelog line "Do not charge maintenance for buildings provided for free by shared wonder effects", post-16869943, page-153 #3053, is about the mod's own shared-wonder feature.)
- **How upkeep is stored (code reading).** Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16429933, page-70 #1381): "each city stores a total maintenance cost"; "Maintenance is only recalculated from scratch when a building granting free improvements is gained or lost." / "Maintenance is not adjusted when techs are unlocked". The full recalculation also runs on a government change (post-16431488, #1384: "the game recalculates all maintenance costs for a player when they change governments"), and it skips obsolete buildings. Unit upkeep is (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "max(0,(N-F-K)*C), where N is the total number of units that player has, F is the no. of free units from gov't and difficulty, K is the no. of king-flagged and captured units, and C is the maintenance cost per unit from gov't type" (post-16398188, page-64 #1264); section 5 §5.3 has the same rule from C3X's code.
- **Unit upkeep** in gold is a separate cost (free units per town/city/metro per government; Republic 2 gold and Feudalism 3 gold per extra unit). Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Unit_Support` and GOVT entries. It is covered in the units section of this compendium.
- **When the treasury cannot pay, buildings and units are lost.** The game messages show that a building is dropped when its maintenance cannot be paid, and a unit is disbanded when its support cannot be paid. Sources:
  - [SCRIPT] `#MAINTSHORT`: "We can no longer support our $IMPROVEMENT0 at $CITY1. We must think more about our treasury!"
  - [SCRIPT] `#NOSUPPORT`: "We have insufficient gold to continue supporting all our units. ^One $UNIT1 unit will be disbanded."
  - [MANUAL] p.139: the treasury is drawn down "to cover the deficit".
  - **Correction (third round, code reading): the sell-then-disband-then-Wealth order is the C3X mod's option, not stock.** The [C3X-INI] text quoted in earlier rounds ("If a player can't afford to pay maintenance on their buildings and units, the game will forcibly lower expenses and raise money by (in order): 1. Selling buildings ... 2. Disbanding units, excluding free ones 3. Switching cities to building Wealth", and the AI alternation and research cut) is the description of the mod setting `aggressively_penalize_bankruptcy`, which defaults to `false`. The C3X `changelog.txt` (Release 14) lists it as a new feature: "Option to aggressively penalize bankrupt players" / "Sells non-gold-generating buildings, non-free units, and switches city production to Wealth", and a later release adds that it "now alternates between selling buildings or disbanding units first for bankrupt AI players and every third turn cuts their research spending". In `injected_code.c` (`patch_Leader_pay_unit_maintenance`) the stock `Leader_pay_unit_maintenance` runs whenever the option is off, and the order is implemented only in the mod's `charge_maintenance_with_aggressive_penalties`. So these steps are not evidence for stock 1.22.
  - What the code does show for stock (C3X-SRC, Flintlock, C3C 1.22): the stock routine pays building and unit upkeep in a random order. The mod's comment says the building-maintenance sum "is called in two places as part of the randomization of whether improv or unit maintenance gets paid first". The stock search for a unit to disband (`find_unsupported_unit`) skips only King units: the mod has to make support-free units look like Kings so that they are not disbanded under its own option (`patch_Unit_has_king_ability_for_find_unsupported`). Cutting a human's research to avoid bankruptcy is also a mod feature ("Automatically cut research spending to avoid bankruptcy", `cut_research_spending_to_avoid_bankruptcy`). The AI's stock `ai_adjust_sliders` runs every turn and "will already cut AI spending but maybe not all the way to zero" (mod comment in `adjust_sliders_preproduction`).
  - So the stock rule is what the game messages above say: a building is lost when its upkeep can't be paid (`#MAINTSHORT`) and a unit is disbanded when support can't be paid (`#NOSUPPORT`).
  - **What is lost, from Flintlock's code readings ([C3X-THREAD]).** Buildings: "There's a method in the game whose job is to delete a building from a random city" / "The buildings are simply deleted." (post-16789620, page-134 #2669), so no gold is paid for them. Units: "The game will only disband at most one unit per turn due to failure to pay maintenance." and (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "it will continue to gain units even after it can't pay for them." (post-16606869, page-103 #2042). Which unit (the stock routine for disbanding on bankruptcy, which C3X reuses): "The game tries to pick the least valuable unit to disband", avoiding captured (upkeep-free) units, King units and armies, with ties broken by list order (post-16356899, page-55 #1081). Later he lists what the search skips: "I can see that the function skips over units with the "king" and "flag unit" abilities at least." (post-16871456, page-154 #3073), and calls it "surprisingly complicated" with a gap: "Someone wrote all those conditions but forgot about the requires support flag." (post-16874607, #3084). So a unit type without `RequireSupport` can still be disbanded (a stock bug). This also refines the C3X-SRC reading above that the search "skips only King units": the valuation keeps captured units and armies at the back as well. Open question 7 is answered except for how the random city's building is picked.
- **Obsolete buildings keep costing upkeep (stock bug).** [C3X-INI]: "Players are not supposed to pay maintenance for obsolete city improvements however they often do because the game does not recompute maintenance after a player unlocks a technology that obsoletes a city improvement." Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16441638, page-71 #1419): "Firaxis intended for obsolete buildings to be maintenance-free". The intended rule (no upkeep once obsolete) is the one to follow. The related exploit: "after triggering a recomputation of maintenance, players can then sell their obsolete buildings to have the maintenance deducted again, effectively getting credited for the cost of maintenance".
- **Selling buildings.** Any building that is not a wonder can be sold from the City Display. [MANUAL] p.143: "Any improvement that is not a Wonder can be sold." and "You cannot sell Wonders of the World." The sale price is not given in the manual. Aqueducts, Hospitals and Small Wonders can't be sold in stock 1.22: [C3X-INI] offers, as mod options, "Allows you to sell aqueducts and hospitals" and "Allows you to sell Small Wonders".

## 8. Pollution and global warming

- **Causes**: some buildings, city population, nuclear meltdowns, volcanic eruptions and nuclear explosions. Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Pollution`: "It is caused by certain city improvements, the meltdown of a $LINK<Nuclear Plant=BLDG_Nuclear_Plant>, $LINK<volcanic eruptions=GCON_Volcanic_Eruptions>, nuclear explosions, and large city populations."
- **Building pollution** ([BIQ] BLDG `Pollution`): Factory 2, Manufacturing Plant 2, Coal Plant 2, Offshore Platform 2, Iron Works 4, Research Lab 1, Airport 1, Commercial Dock 1. Hydro, Nuclear and Solar plants are 0.
- **Population pollution**: 1 point per citizen above 12. Source: CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "For each population point you have above 12, you will get 1 pollution 'point'". https://apolyton.net/forum/miscellaneous/archives/civ3-strategy-archive/54339-pollution (roadcage) adds a possible tech dependence: "The threshold appears to be size 12 during the industrial age."
- **Stock bug above size 12.** Antal1987's v1 patch notes (in [C3X-NOTES] `Antal's Hex Patches.txt`, C3C 1.22): "Fix of annoying non-zero pollution (when city size > 12) even if the city has mass transit and recycling center." So in stock 1.22 a city above 12 keeps some pollution with both buildings. This fits the FAQ's "caps population pollution at 1" and Catt's "still have only 2 pollution triangles" below: the cap is 1, not 0.
- **Population pollution is size minus the city size cap (C3X re-implementation).** C3X's replacement for the stock function (https://github.com/maxpetul/C3X/blob/master/injected_code.c, `patch_City_get_pollution_from_pop`) computes `base_pollution = this->Body.Population.Size - p_bic_data->General.MaximumSize_City`, then returns 1 if a building with the "Removes Population Pollution" flag is active, else the full amount. The building side (`patch_City_get_pollution_from_buildings`) returns 1 when a "Reduces Buildings Pollution" building is active and the total is positive. This is the mod's code (it calls the stock function when its `enable_negative_pop_pollution` option is off), so it is evidence of how the author read the stock logic, not a disassembly. It ties the threshold to the BIQ's City size limit (12), not to a tech, which answers roadcage's tech question (open question 8) in favour of no tech term.
- **The stock pollution function (disassembly, Antal1987, C3C 1.22).** https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/how-to-reduce-pollution-at-all-in-civ3-conquests.526144/ #1 (14 May 2014): "In Civ3Conquests v1.22 function class_City::Calculate_Pollution has address 0x004B1CA0." / "total pollution = max(0, population_pollution) + max(0, buildings_pollution)". His pseudocode: `if (City.Size < General_Settings.Max_City_Size) population_pollution = 0; else { population_pollution = City.Size - General_Settings.Max_City_Size; if(City.Has_Improvement_With_Flag(Removes_Population_Pollution)) population_pollution = 1; }` and `buildings_pollution = sum{Improvement_.Pollution where City.Has_Improvement(i)}`, set to 1 if positive and the city has the reducing building. (The fetch shows the building test also named `Removes_Population_Pollution`, which looks like a slip for the "Reduces Building Pollution" flag, the Recycling Center's.) "General_Settings.Max_City_Size is a standard scenario parameter" / "By default it is 12." So the threshold is the rule-file city size limit, with no tech term (open question 8). The "1" is a hard-coded constant ("Address of the constant (1) is: 0x004B1D61"), which his patch sets to 0. Read literally, the pseudocode also gives a size-12 city with Mass Transit 1 population pollution instead of 0, because the `else` branch runs when Size equals the limit. This is the stock-bug item above: the engine follows the intended rule (Mass Transit and Recycling Center remove their share). The thread doesn't give the per-turn chance or where pollution lands.
- **Buildings given by wonders don't pollute.** [C3X-INI] option: "Causes improvements granted for free by great or small wonders to contribute pollution as though they had been built in the city." So in stock they add none. Flintlock gives the mechanism ([C3X-THREAD] post-16515622, page-80 #1589): "Cities store a running count of building pollution that is increased when a building is built" and "Buildings that are granted for free don't go through the same process as those that are physically built". Negative pollution values (used only by mods) cancel building pollution, not population pollution (post-16236956, page-31 #615).
- **Pollution never lands on water (code reading).** Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16083731, page-9 #179): "It is hardcoded that pollution can't spawn on water"; the code "skips water tiles when searching for a spot for pollution to spawn" (post-16635376, page-108 #2147). Nanuk (post-16083684, #178): impassable terrain does not stop it, which agrees with [C3X-INI] above.
- **Mitigation**: Mass Transit cuts population pollution to 1, and Recycling Center cuts building pollution to 1. Sources:
  - CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "building Mass Transit (which caps population pollution at 1)".
  - https://apolyton.net/forum/miscellaneous/archives/civ3-play-the-world/82366-how-does-pollution-occur (Catt): "Recycling Centers (available with Recycling) eliminates all but one of the pollution triangles caused by city improvements." and "a city could be size 40, with every possible polluting improvement and still have only 2 pollution triangles."
  - [BIQ] flags: Mass Transit `RemovesPopulationPollution`, Recycling Center `ReducesBuildingPollution`.
- **Effect**: each turn, the pollution points ("triangles") give a chance that a random tile in the radius becomes polluted. The exact percentage is UNVERIFIED (open question 8). Sources:
  - same Apolyton thread (Catt): "The pollution triangles represent the probability that a random city radius tile will be hit by pollution each turn".
  - [MANUAL] p.88: "The number of symbols corresponds to the probability each turn of a square within the city radius becoming polluted."
  - The only figure found is a hedged recollection. Skanky Burns, Apolyton 54339 (vanilla, Feb 2002): "2 tons of pollution, IIRC giving you a 2% chance". That suggests 1% per point, but it is not tested. A second, also untested, statement: HellboundHeathcliff, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/help-with-civ-3-regular-version.119983 #5 (2005, vanilla question): "One symbol represents 1% chance of pollution appearing in that city radius each turn." brennan (#8) adds "a city with both structures [Mass Transit and Recycling Center] has a 2% chance of suffering pollution per turn". The inventory's "1% per pollution icon" thread (CFC 251335) is about Civ1.
- **Whether shield output itself pollutes**: disputed (Disputed 15). [MANUAL] p.88 names cities that are "excessively productive (produces lots of shields)". roadcage (Apolyton 54339) instead models Civ3 as "pollution = f3(facilities, pop, tech)", against Civ2's "f2(shields, pop, tech)". The C3C [BIQ] and [PEDIA] list only buildings and population.
- **Where it lands, and the citizen on it.** A citizen on a tile that becomes polluted is moved to a specialist. [C3X-INI]: "keeps citizens working the tiles they've been assigned to when pollution spawns there instead of converting them to specialists" (a mod option). Pollution can land on impassable tiles in stock ("Stops the game from placing pollution on impassable tiles" is a mod option). [C3X-INI]'s `pollution_spawn_effect` describes stock as "standard", against mod modes that reduce population. The tile search starts at a random place in the city radius. C3X-SRC (`patch_rand_int_to_place_pollution`): "Intercept the call where the game gets a random index at which to start searching for a suitable tile to become polluted. Normally, it passes 20 as the limit here." So stock rolls a start among the 20 radius tiles and takes the first suitable tile from there; what counts as "suitable" is not stated. The mod's own population-loss mode rolls `rand_int(100) < pollution` (1% per pollution point). That is the mod's rule, so it does not settle the stock chance (open question 8).
- **Cleanup**: Workers use "Clear Damage". Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Worker_Jobs`: "{Clear Damage (Shift-C)} ^Removes $LINK<pollution=GCON_Pollution>, including fallout from a $LINK<volcanic eruption=GCON_Volcanic_Eruptions>, and $LINK<craters=GCON_Craters>."
- **What a polluted tile yields**: disputed (section 7, Disputed 6). [MANUAL] p.89: "It reduces the production of food, raw materials, and commerce income to zero. Once the terrain is detoxified, production returns to pre-pollution levels." [PEDIA] `#GCON_Craters` says craters, "Like pollution", reduce output "by one". Apolyton 48566 (Dec 2001) has both claims, untested: Magician says "Polluted tile produce nothing but waste", and Trachmyr says "you still get the FOOD from the tile (that is polluted)".
- **Nuclear meltdown** (tested, PTW 1.21f). It can happen only after a Nuclear Plant city has spent a full turn in disorder, and the chance is lower than the manual says. Catt, CFC power-plant article: "the nuclear plant will never melt down on the turn that the host city enters into disorder" and "empirical testing indicates that the correct changes are about 10% - 15%" (against the manual's 50%). [MANUAL] p.89: a meltdown destroys "half of the city's population".
- **Terrain decay** from long-standing pollution. Per-terrain `PollutionEffect` in [BIQ]: Grassland→Plains, Plains→Desert. Forest and Jungle point to index 14, read as "Base Terrain Type". Marsh points to index 11, which is Coast in the BIQ terrain order. This is how the values read; check it. Source: [EDHELP] "Pollution Effect (Terrain Properties)": "Selects the terrain type (if any) that a tile of the selected terrain type will become if pollution stays on that tile for an extended length of time. For example, grassland would become plains, and plains would become desert."
- **Global warming**: world pollution (all city pollution plus nukes plus meltdowns) can trigger terrain loss. The trigger threshold and the number of tiles hit are UNVERIFIED (open question 9). Sources:
  - [PEDIA] `#GCON_Pollution`: "If world pollution reaches sufficiently critical levels, there is a chance that global warming will occur. It can cause forests and jungles to disappear, and grasslands to become plains, and plains to become desert. World pollution is the sum of all city pollution, nuclear explosions, and meltdowns."
  - [RM129] 1.16f: "The Sun Icon on the Info Box represents the amount of global warming affecting the game. If the sun does not display, there is insufficient global warming to affect the terrain." It goes on: "Once the sun displays, the brighter it is, the greater the impact of global warming."
  - [SCRIPT] has three messages: `#GLOBALWARMING_FOREST_JUNGLE` ("Global Warming" has destroyed $TERRAIN0!), `#GLOBALWARMING` ("Global Warming has turned $TERRAIN0 into $TERRAIN1!") and `#GLOBALWARMING_RISING_COASTLINES` ("Coastlines are rising!"). The last one fits the BIQ Marsh→Coast `PollutionEffect` (open question 9). The same three keys are in the vanilla and PTW `script.txt`.
- **What drives warming, by player report.** Pollution on the ground is said not to count; the risk follows the world's city pollution icons and nukes, from every civ. WarpStorm, https://codehappy.net/apolyton/threads/60991-1.htm (vanilla, Sep 2002), second-hand: "Firaxis has said that polution on the ground does not affect global warming." Also: "The risk of global warming is proportional to the little triangles in the cities of the world" "and the number of nukes dropped", and "Global warming in Civ3 is caused by all civs not just yours." alexman, same thread: "I believe the color of the sun depends on the total number of yellow polution triangles". This matches [PEDIA]'s "World pollution is the sum of all city pollution, nuclear explosions, and meltdowns."
- **What warming does in practice.** Forests and jungles are removed and terrain is downgraded. NJRBSA88, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/global-warming-doesnt-work.539761/ (Civ3 Complete, 2014): "global warming only worked insofar that it eliminated forests and jungles." DRoseDARs, codehappy 60991: "Grasslands near Such'N'Such City have turned into Plains due to Global Warming". The CFC FAQ (https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/): "every few turns some grassland tiles might turn into plains, and plains into desert." The trigger level and the tiles per event are still UNVERIFIED. C3X-THREAD posters lean to forest and jungle only: churchillpines (post-16961916, page-184 #3677, hedged): "It appears that global warming only affects forest and jungle tiles", "This is also the case with Civ III Conquests version 1.22"; Android Assembly (post-16824337, page-137 #2726) says the game does not make the BIQ's terrain changes and calls that a bug; tjs282 (post-16824434, #2727) mostly sees deforestation to base terrain, though he has seen "Grass burnt to Plains (and Plains to Desert) due to nuclear fallout". If the BIQ changes are skipped in 1.22, that is a shipped bug; the intended rule is the [PEDIA] one above.

## 9. Golden Ages

- **Length**: 20 turns (editable; 0 disables Golden Ages). Source: [BIQ] `GoldenAgeDuration=20`. [EDHELP] "Duration (Golden Age General Setting)": "Setting this to zero will disable the triggering of any Golden Age. Default duration is 20 turns."
  - Code (Antal1987, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/infinite-golden-age-and-science-age.526292/ #1, May 2014, C3C 1.22 memory layout): the age is a single per-player field, "Golden Age End. It's 4-byte integer and it represents the exact turn, when Golden Age disables", set to "Current Turn ... + Golden Age Duration". C3X's header has the same field (`Leader.Golden_Age_End`), and its display code treats the age as active while `current_turn < Golden_Age_End`. No separate on/off flag exists, so a second trigger during an age can only move the end turn. Whether it does is not shown. The Age of Science is separate ("it sets Science Age Status to 1 and Science Age End to Current Turn + 20"); see section 4.
- **Effect**: +1 shield and/or +1 commerce on worked tiles already producing at least 1. Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Golden_Age`: "every citizen laborer in the empire is more productive, producing one extra $LINK<shield=GCON_Shields> and/or $LINK<commerce=GCON_Commerce> if already producing at least one."
- **The Despotism penalty still applies** (section 1).
- **Triggers**:
  - **Unique unit victory.** The civ's unique unit wins a battle against another civ; barbarians don't count. [PEDIA] `#GCON_Golden_Age`: "Victories versus barbarians will not initiate a Golden Age in this way."
  - **Wonders.** The civ builds Great Wonder(s) covering *both* of its traits. [PEDIA] `#GCON_Golden_Age`: "If a civilization builds a Great Wonder (or Wonders) that corresponds to [both] its unique $LINK<strengths=GCON_Strengths>, its Golden Age can be triggered." Each wonder's Civilopedia entry lists the traits it counts for (e.g. Pyramids: "Industrious, Religious, and Agricultural"). [RM129] (1.16f additions): "If you build sufficient wonders that match your civilization themes, a Golden Age will trigger."
- **The wonder check runs only when the civ completes a wonder (any wonder).** Captured wonders count toward the traits, but they trigger nothing until the civ builds a wonder itself. Sources:
  - Bamspeedy's C3C list, CFC 70063 (#47, "not bugs"): "the game checks the qualifications for a golden age everytime you build a wonder (any wonder)."
  - Puppeteer, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/golden-age-triggers-by-great-wonders.411703/ (C3C, 2011): "Capture or build a needed wonder, capture another needed wonder, self-build any wonder = Golden Age".
  - bouncelot, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/great-wonder-list-to-trigger-golden-ages.99092: "if you capture the wonders that will would give you a golden age if you had built them and then build any other wonder you will get a golden age."
  - Two wonders that each match one trait are enough. [PEDIA] gives the Babylon example ("the Great Library (scientific) [and] the Oracle (religious)"), and the players' lists treat it the same way. This resolves the second half of open question 16.
- **Great Wonder traits (C3C)**, from the [BIQ] BLDG trait flags read with QueryCiv3. They match CommandoBob's table in CFC 411703.

  | Trait | Great Wonders |
  |---|---|
  | Agricultural | Pyramids, Hanging Gardens, Universal Suffrage, Hoover Dam, Cure for Cancer |
  | Commercial | Colossus, Great Lighthouse, Magellan's, Smith's, United Nations |
  | Expansionist | Colossus, Great Lighthouse, Magellan's, Copernicus', SETI |
  | Industrious | Pyramids, Hanging Gardens, Great Wall, Hoover Dam, Manhattan Project |
  | Militaristic | Great Wall, Sun Tzu, Leonardo's, Manhattan Project, Statue of Zeus, Knights Templar |
  | Religious | Pyramids, Oracle, Sistine Chapel, JS Bach's, Temple of Artemis, Statue of Zeus, Knights Templar |
  | Scientific | Great Library, Newton's, Theory of Evolution, Cure for Cancer, Longevity, Mausoleum |
  | Seafaring | Colossus, Great Lighthouse, Magellan's, Smith's, Mausoleum |

  The Internet carries all eight traits, and Shakespeare's Theatre none. Small wonders carry flags in the BIQ too (Heroic Epic: Religious, Seafaring; Forbidden Palace: Religious; Military Academy: Militaristic; and others), but a War Academy guide (section 4, `?p=151`) says small wonders cannot trigger a Golden Age.
- **Version difference: the vanilla/PTW trait lists differ.** geowhiz1, CFC 99092: "This list is for vanilla & PTW", for example "The Colossus = Expansionist, Religious, Commercial", "The Pyramids = Industrious, Religious" and "Magellan's Voyage = Expansionist, Commercial". thetrooper, same thread: "The Colossus was instant GA for the expansionist/religious (matching The Iroquois) combo in vanilla." Agricultural and Seafaring exist only in C3C, as do Temple of Artemis, Statue of Zeus, Mausoleum and Knights Templar.
- **One Golden Age per civ per game.** Source: CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "A civilization can only have 1 in a game."
- **Age of Science** is separate: a Scientific Leader starts 20 turns of +25% research. Source: [PEDIA] `#GCON_Age_of_Science`: "a 20-turn period in which the $LINK<scientific research=GCON_Research> of each city in that civilization's empire is 25% more productive." The C3C changes list (CFC 104294, post 13) reports it broken: "this functionality does not work." Section 4 §4.8 confirms it from the code (Antal1987, C3X-INI), and Flintlock lists it among stock bugs ([C3X-THREAD] post-16055720, page-5 #91: "Science age beakers not actually awarded"). The intended +25% is the rule to follow.

## 11. Founding, joining, razing and abandoning cities

- **Settler**: 30 shields, costs 2 population. It founds a city, or joins one for +2 population. Source: [BIQ] PRTO Settler `ShieldCost=30`, `PopulationCost=2`. [PEDIA] `#PRTO_Settler`: "A city that builds a [settler] loses two citizens from its population." Also: "The settler disappears and the city's population gains two citizens."
- **Worker**: 10 shields, costs 1 population. It joins a city for +1 population. Source: [BIQ] Worker `PopulationCost=1`. [PEDIA] `#PRTO_Worker`: "A city that builds a [worker] loses one citizen from its population." Also: "The worker disappears and the city's population gains one citizen."
- **Joining in general**: joining adds the unit's population cost. Workers cannot join a starving city. Sources:
  - [EDHELP] "Join City": "The population cost of the unit will then be added the city population, and the unit will disappear."
  - [RMPTW] 1.01f: "Workers can no longer join starving cities."
  - Whether a join can push a city past its Aqueduct or Hospital cap is UNVERIFIED (open question 11).
- **Founding rules** (terrain): cities can be built on Grassland, Plains, Desert, Flood Plain, Tundra, Hills, Forest and Jungle. They cannot be built on Mountains, Marsh, Volcano or water. Source: [BIQ] TERR `AllowCities`.
- **Minimum distance between cities: no city may be founded next to another**, so at least one tile must lie between them. No editor setting changes this. Sources (section 7 §9):
  - [MANUAL] (Build/Join City, p.~131): "Note that you cannot build cities in terrain squares directly adjacent to an existing city."
  - C3C city-placement article, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/empire-management/city-placement/: the tightest pattern, infinite sprawl, is "CxCxC".
  - Steam thread, https://steamcommunity.com/app/3910/discussions/0/3453716885198230413: "I didn't find an option for that in the editor."
- **Maximum number of cities**: 512. Source: [RM129] 1.16f: "The maximum number of cities permitted in a game is 512, increased from 256." Code reading (Flintlock, [C3X-THREAD]): "There are two points in the game code where it enforces the city limit" and that is "why the game stops you from founding and displays "Too many cities" once you reach the limit" (post-16871456, page-154 #3073); past 512, "Founding more than 512 cities causes the program to write beyond the bounds of the table" (post-16025200, page-1 #16).
- **Minimum spacing, code reading.** Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16569253, page-91 #1808): "Under the standard game rules, the closest you can place cities is at a distance of 2 tiles"; (post-16404684, page-65 #1295) the mod's separation setting is an adjustment to "the base game's minimum separation" of 1. This matches the [MANUAL] rule above.
- **Abandoning**: available from the right-click menu since 1.21f. You can never abandon your last city. Sources:
  - [RM129] 1.21f: "Added "Abandon City" to right-click menu."
  - [RMPTW]: "Fixed a bug where the user could abandon all of their cities if they had more than one."
  - An abandoned city leaves ruins, and the site can be settled again. soul_warrior, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/raise-abandon.113940 (C3C era, 2005): the city "will turn into rubble." Players sell the buildings first. Zelda's Man, same thread: "Sometimes people abandon the city instead of razing it so that they can sell all the improvements in it first."
- **Settler or Worker from a city too small to pay for it.** Player reports differ by version:
  - **Later play (2005):** finishing a Settler in a size-2 city with no food gain brings up a choice to abandon the city and keep the Settler. Pentium, CFC 113940: "It happens when you try to build a settler in 2-size not growing city." TimBentley, same thread: "The city has to have no food gain."
  - **Early vanilla (Jan 2002):** the unit simply waits. Conan, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/getting-rid-of-cities.14157/: Settlers and Workers are "delayed until the size is 2 or 3, as appropriate", and "Building a settler or worker, or anything else cannot even be rushed if this would disband a city."
  - The same holds for a Worker in a size-1 city. The Last Conformist, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/firaxis-corruption-breakdown.73485/page-12 #231 (Jan 2004, C3C beta era): "I just ran a li'l test, and indeed you need need 0fpt to abandon a city by building a Worker." Grille (#226): "it is possible to get rid of a city by worker/settler production."
  - Abandoning was added to the right-click menu in 1.21f (above), which fits the change. The exact size test for the abandon prompt is still UNVERIFIED.
- **Razing**: capturing a size-1 city with no culture razes it automatically. A razed Great Wonder is lost for good. Sources:
  - [RM129] 1.16f: "Capturing a population 1 city that has no culture will automatically raze it."
  - CFC FAQ, https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/: "If that wonder is razed, no other civilization can ever build it again; it is lost forever."
  - [MANUAL] p.73: "If captured by military means, a city becomes yours to raze or to keep."
  - **Razing gives slave Workers (C3C).** Players report this but give no rate per citizen. Boss Tweed, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/occupy-vs-raze.313823: "I typically raze for the slaves (but thats in Conquest only)". zerksees, same thread: "A size 5 will get you two workers IIRC". CommandoBob, same thread: they are "Workers of a different nationality than yours (i.e., a slave)".
  - **Auto-raze applies to the AI too** (community). Erlend Philip Wood ([C3X-THREAD] post-16572215, page-91 #1814): "Under the standard rules, both the human player and the AI players will auto-raze a city if it is" size 1 with no culture; (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "There is no choice in the matter." He puts the AI's own raze choice at about 5-10% of captures (observed).
  - Other raze rules (the exact slave count, and whether the raze choice depends on difficulty or culture) are UNVERIFIED (open question 13).
- **Gold from capturing a city (code reading).** Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16726482, page-124 #2469): "If it's the last city your opponent has, you get their entire treasury" / (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "Otherwise, the amount of gold equals their treasury divided by their city count multiplied by a factor" / "That factor is 0.5 for cities of level 1 (towns), 0.75 for level 2, and 1.0 for level 3 (metropolises)". A unit ability can double it: (paraphrase from a summarised fetch) "Already in the base game using the official editor, you can give units the ability to double gold captured from cities" (post-16894490, page-162 #3222); no stock unit has it.
- **Drafting tests the size level, not the number 7 (code reading).** Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16833134 and post-16833692, page-141 #2806/#2810): "cities must be at least of level 2 size in order to draft." / "it's actually checking that the city is at least size level 2." With the default BIQ that is size 7 (Disputed 12).

## 12. Capitals and palaces

- **One Palace per civ.** It marks the capital and has the "Center of Empire" flag. Source: [PEDIA] `#BLDG_Palace`: "Each civilization can have only one palace at a time." [EDHELP] "Center of Empire": "the center of the empire is set to the city in which the improvement is built." Building it elsewhere moves the capital. The exact handling of the old Palace (removed vs sold) is UNVERIFIED.
- **Palace cost (since 1.16f)** depends on the number of cities: `PC = C × 600 / OCN`, rounded down to a multiple of 100, clamped to 300-1000 shields. On a Standard map that is 300 for up to 13 cities, 400 for 14-16, and 1000 for 34 or more. Sources:
  - [RM129] 1.16f: "The cost of building a Palace is now based on the number of cities in your civilization."
  - https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/game-mechanics/cost-of-the-palace-for-c3c/: "PC = (C * 600) / OCN" and "The palace can never cost less than 300 shields and never more than 1000 shields".
  - Only one city can build the Palace at a time. [RM129] 1.16f: "Palaces and spaceship parts can only be built in one city at a time."
- **Free palace jump**: when the capital is razed or abandoned, the Palace moves for free to the highest-scoring city in the empire. Ties go to the first city in the database. Source: https://civfanatics.com/civ3/strategy/game-mechanics/free-palace-jump/ and the forum thread https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/free-palace-jump.47304/ (DaveMcW, Mar 2003, tested on PTW, about 1.21):
  - "When the capital city is razed or abandonned, each city in the empire scores".
  - "3 points per national citizen", "1 point per foreign citizen", "1 point per neighboring town (1-6)", "2 points per neighboring city (7-12)", "3 points per neighboring metropolis (13+)", "1 point per military unit". Buildings do not count.
  - "A neighbor city must yours, and within 8 tiles walking distance from the target city (a 17x17 square)." The radius is measured from each *candidate* city, not from the old capital. DaveMcW (47304 p.2): "It's simple walking distance, making the effective area a 17x17 square." "The old capital does not count as a neighbor."
  - Ties go to "the first city in the database, which is usually the oldest city" (p.2).
  - The weights were revised during testing (p.2, Jul 2003): "Cities (7-12) are worth 2 towns (1-6), and metropolises (13+) are worth 3 towns", and every value was multiplied by 3 to clear fractions.
  - justanick (https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/looking-for-the-formula-on-how-the-palace-gets-relocated.703374/, 2026, C3C) repeats this scoring as "the exact formula" after "weeks of testing".
  - Whether a *captured* capital also jumps is UNVERIFIED: none of these sources covers capture. A civ can never be left without a capital: [RM129] 1.17f: "Fixed bug which allowed civs with no capitals."

## 13. End-of-turn (interturn) processing order

No Firaxis description exists. This is the community reconstruction (C3C), from Lanzelot's thread https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/interturn-mechanics.443195/ (Oct 2011, 3 pages) and its corrections. The fetch tool would not give the first post verbatim, so the list below is paraphrased.

- **Phase 1, global:**
  1. Worker jobs finish ("1 turn" jobs complete now).
  2. AI units move.
  3. Commerce for all of the human player's cities is collected (gold and beakers), upkeep is paid, and finished techs are granted. Lanzelot was unsure whether AI commerce is collected here.
- **Phase 2, per city, in city order:**
  1. Happiness is checked, and unhappy cities riot.
  2. Food is collected and growth applied, with the governor placing the new citizen.
  3. Shields are collected and finished items are delivered.
  4. Culture is added and borders expand.
- **Phase 3, global:** units that did not move heal, then the AI production phase runs, then the score is updated. Lanzelot (#6), after testing: "score calculation takes place after the build phase".
- **Gold and research come before the city pass (code reading).** Flintlock ([C3X-THREAD] post-16122366, page-15 #300): "the game calculates gold and research first then does per-city things including pollution". tjs282 (post-16021595, page-1 #11): "Pollution definitely happens before production is calculated". So pollution falls in the per-city phase, before shields (open question 18).
- **Commerce comes before shields and food.** alexman, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/production-phase-micromanagement-a-powerful-exploit.63168/ (PTW era, Sep 2003): "Commerce for all cities gets added up before the shield and food production is calculated." His tile-reuse exploit shows that the city screen can be changed between cities' production steps. He reported it as a bug. No fix is recorded, and players treat it as a banned exploit.
- **Corrections and additions from the thread:**
  - **City order is internal ID order, not strictly founding order.** IDs are reused, so the two can differ. justanick (#29): "That is not exactly true. Every city has an intern 9 bit number".
  - **Effects of a wonder or Golden Age that begins mid-pass apply to the cities processed after it.** justanick (#31): "All cities after that will profit from the higher production".
  - **Tourism and Great People income are ordinary commerce in step 3.** PaperBeetle (#27): "Yes, this is regular commerce, just like the stuff that comes from your tiles."
  - **Luxury affects mood in the city phase. Spending happens in the commerce phase.** albutrosv13 (#34): "the effect of luxury tax(also entertainers) comes in the production phase".
  - **A new wonder's commerce effect waits a turn.** Spoonwood (#36): The Internet's research bonus lags because "commerce was already calculated before completion of it". He also says happiness is checked before wonders complete ("the happiness calculation phase has already completed").
  - **Disease, pollution, volcanoes and global warming are missing from the list.** kaskavel (#40, 2026): "Disease strikes immediately after commerce is collected and before the city is checked for happiness." Also: "Pollution is also missing from the OP." He suggests the order pollution, disease, happiness, untested. kaskavel (#41) adds "Volcanos" and "Global warming" as missing steps.
  - **Disbanding during the interturn adds shields.** pol1 (#14): "Disbanding can add some shields to the bin." Lanzelot (#15) says such a disband still costs that turn's upkeep.
- **Status:** the steps above are a community reconstruction, and only the score timing and commerce-before-production have been tested. Pollution is in the per-city phase (Flintlock, above); where disease, starvation and the end of a Golden Age fall is UNVERIFIED.

## Disputed

1. **Despotism penalty wording.** [PEDIA] and the CFC FAQ say any of food, shields *or commerce* above 2 drops by 1 (so 3→2, 4→3). [EDHELP] says "no tile may generate more than 2 food and shields". That wording reads as a hard cap at 2 and leaves out commerce. Firaxis' own Civilopedia and the FAQ agree on −1 including commerce.
2. **Republic/Democracy trade bonus condition.** The CFC Civilopedia mirror says "+1 commerce to any tile already producing at least 1". [EDHELP] says "each tile generates an additional +1 to commerce", which reads as unconditional.
3. **Forbidden Palace and distance (C3C 1.20+).** alexman (C3C article and thread): the FP counts as a second palace for *distance*. It also lowers rank corruption everywhere through the OCN bonus (Gp·Nwe). alexman, CFC 76619 p.4 (#61): "rank corruption is reduced everywhere because of the OCN bonus from the FP/SPHQ". Roland Johansen (p.2): "both rank and distance corruption get affected by building the forbidden palace", and under Communism the FP has no distance effect. Against this, [RMC3C] 1.20: "Updated Civilopedia.txt Forbidden Palace to explain that all cities are still affected by distance from the Real Capital." Also against it, AbuHab (p.3) quotes the readme ("Forbidden Palace cities no longer act as a 2nd city empire"), and the C3C changes list (Arathorn): "The Forbidden Palace no longer provides a second core like a palace." alexman answered the readme directly (p.3, #50): "Of course the readme is wrong!" Lean: alexman's model (FP = distance reference point plus an OCN bonus, with rank measured from the Palace only), which also matches Tavis' stated fix (§5).
4. **Optimal number of cities, Standard map. RESOLVED (version difference).** [EDHELP] "Optimal Number of Cities": "On a standard 100 x 100 map, the default is 16." That is the vanilla/PTW value ([BIQ-V], [BIQ-PTW]: Standard 16). C3C [BIQ] uses 20 (§5).
5. **Wealth conversion rate. RESOLVED (version difference).** 8:1 in vanilla/PTW and 4:1 in C3C, halved by Economics in each ([BIQ-V]/[BIQ-PTW] 8, [BIQ] 4; [PEDIA-V]/[PEDIA-PTW] "eight to one", [PEDIA] "four to one"). [EDHELP]'s "Default is 8" is the old default (§3).
6. **Military Great Leaders hurrying.** [PEDIA] `#GCON_Leaders`: an MGL can hurry "an Improvement or Small Wonder". [ED-SUPP]: "NOTE: Military leaders can not rush." That note sits next to the Allow Scientific Leaders rule and may only mean Great Wonders. The C3C changes list: "Military Great Leaders (MGLs) can no longer rush Great Wonders." [C3X-INI] (code reading) supports "no Great Wonders" and shows an AI-only bug: its replacement leader AI "fixes a bug that allowed the AI to rush any kind of city production, including units and (with an MGL) wonders", and it offers `allow_military_leaders_to_hurry_wonders` (default off) as a mod option. So in stock 1.22 a human's MGL can't hurry Great Wonders, while the AI's leader routine can hurry anything. Third round, code (C3X-SRC `patch_Unit_can_hurry_production`): the stock check `Unit_can_hurry_production (unit, city, exclude_cheap_improvements)` depends on the leader's kind. To let an MGL hurry wonders, the mod relabels it `LK_Scientific` for the call and then sets it back to `LK_Military`. [C3X-INI] also says that leaders placed in a scenario without a kind "are allowed to hurry production of great wonders and spaceship parts", which the mod fixes "so they behave like normal MGLs". So a normal MGL is barred from **spaceship parts** as well as Great Wonders. The mod reads the second parameter as "exclude cheap improvements", which suggests the routine can skip low-cost builds. Who sets it is not stated. It does not say whether human MGLs hurry improvements, but "any kind of city production, including units" reads as wider than the intended set, which fits [PEDIA] (improvements and Small Wonders).
7. **Granary at 6→7 and 12→13.** Bamspeedy: "When you hit size 7 or size 13, the granary is emptied". MadScot: Firaxis "decided to zero out the granary instead". Both are at https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/granaries-dont-work.53078/. Against them: Hygro in the same thread ("you DO keep the food in the granary box") and Spoonwood's save test at https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/pottery-is-underrated.683382/ ("changing from size 12 to 13 keeps 20 food in the box"). Not resolved in this pass. Spoonwood's later save test is the strongest evidence. A C3C thread splits the same way: Tomoyo, https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/is-this-granary-bug.113027/ #2: "Whenever a city grows to size 7 or 13, the granary empties."; Zakharov, #4: "the granary doesn't empty, it just grows in capacity by 10 food", the size-6 granary's food carries over but "it requires 20 food (an extra 10) in the box before it is full again", and at 13 "20 food is transferred to the new box but 30 (an extra 10) is required". Zakharov's reading explains why the box *looks* emptied and agrees with Spoonwood and Hygro.
8. **Smith's Trading Company coverage.** [RM129] (1.16f additions): Smith's pays "all trade-based city improvements (Marketplaces, Banks, Harbors, and Airports) instead of all improvements with a maintenance cost of one gold". The C3C [PEDIA] adds Commercial Dock and Stock Exchange. The coverage follows the version.
9. **Pop-rush history.** The 1.17f Realms Beyond guide says "For 1 to 39 shields remaining ... the cost is the same, one citizen". The [RM129] 1.21f fix made the first citizen 20 shields. For the target patches, use 20 per citizen. Hurry and draft unhappiness: 40 turns in 1.17f, back to 20 in 1.21f ([RM129]).
10. **Founding on forest/jungle.** inca911 says the tile becomes grassland. Vipsanius says clearing forest gives plains. Both are at https://apolyton.net/forum/miscellaneous/archives/civ3-strategy-archive/42626-distilled-civ3-tips-and-notes. Lean: the tile reverts to its own base terrain (§1 city-tile bullet: piratebrun, Nero Would, Chicken Pizza). That is grassland only when the forest or jungle sits on grassland.
11. **City-tile commerce for Commercial civs.** The German CivWiki gives +2 at size 7-12 and +3 at 13 or more, but its own maximum sum does not fit that table. Section 8's CFC FAQ and Sullla describe +1 at City and +2 at Metropolis ("2 extra gold for each metropolis"). Lean: +1/+2, the two independent sources.
12. **Draft size. RESOLVED (§11):** [PEDIA] `#GCON_Draft`: "a population of 7 or higher". The CFC FAQ (https://civfanatics.com/civ3/faq/, "What is the army draft?"): the city "has to have at least 6 population". Flintlock's code reading ([C3X-THREAD] page-141 #2810) shows the test is "at least size level 2", which is 7 or more under the default `MaximumLevel1CitySize=6`. [PEDIA] is right.
13. **Corruption rounding (pre-C3C).** alexman (CFC 19922): "round to the nearest integer" (verified by DaviddesJ). The Apolyton Corruption FAQ (codehappy 56739, alexman, 2002) says the 95% cap is applied with the result "rounded down" (wording as summarised by the fetch tool). The C3C article says "rounded to the closest integer". DaviddesJ (19922 page 5 #85, with test examples) and alexman (page 7 #134) both say nearest. Firaxis' rank code (Tavis, 73485 page 7 #124) halves with `+ 1) / 2`, an integer round-half-up, but the excerpt stops before the final division. alexman himself warned that his C3C formula is better for comparing cities "rather than for predicting the exact number of beakers and gold lost to corruption", because "it's difficult to figure out all the internal rounding in the intermediate calculations in the Civ3 game engine" (https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/understand-your-cities-corruption-better-and-tackle-it.88390/ #2). Lean: nearest.
14. **Tourism and corruption (C3C).** The changes list (CFC 104294): tourist revenue is "uncorrupted, not affected by buildings". CKS (CFC 443195 p.2): "Tourist income is, indeed, corrupted." MAS (CFC 242296): it is added "before corruption". Not resolved.
15. **Shield-based pollution.** [MANUAL] p.88: pollution hits a city "that is excessively productive (produces lots of shields)". roadcage (Apolyton 54339): Civ3 pollution depends on "facilities, pop, tech", not on shields as in Civ2. The [BIQ] has only per-building `Pollution` values and the population rule. Lean: no shield term in C3C (buildings plus population above 12). HellboundHeathcliff (CFC 119983 #9) agrees: "Pollution in civ3 is not tied in any way to shield production" and "A factory ALWAYS produced 2 pollution icons no matter how many shields the city is producing". The C3X mod's re-implemented pollution functions (§8) also have only buildings and population. *Resolved in the third round (disassembly):* Antal1987's reading of the 1.22 `class_City::Calculate_Pollution` (CFC 526144 #1, §8) is "total pollution = max(0, population_pollution) + max(0, buildings_pollution)", with no shield term.

## UNVERIFIED / open questions

Numbers are kept stable because other sections refer to them. Resolved items say where the answer now lives.

1. **Water and lake tile yields.** The BIQ reads Coast 1/0/2, Sea 1/0/1, Ocean 0/0/0. [PEDIA] lists Coastal and Sea at 1 food and a fresh-water lake at 2 food, and gives no Ocean food. The Ocean value and the lake food bonus need checking in game (also section 7, open questions 12 and 13).
2. **Railroad bonus. RESOLVED (§1):** +1 food on irrigated tiles and +1 shield on mined tiles, from community tests (section 7 §4.3).
3. **Production switching penalty. RESOLVED (§3):** none. [MANUAL] pp.61 and 141 say only the overrun is lost. C3C Civil Engineer shields are held back from units.
4. **Gold hurry cost by item type. RESOLVED (§4):** the same 4 gold per shield (8 from an empty box) for units and improvements. [MANUAL] p.144 gives "four times", and every unit example fits that rate. No Civ3 source shows a unit premium or a non-linear term. Strictly, this is an absence of evidence and not a Firaxis statement.
5. **Pre-C3C corruption formula. RESOLVED (§5):** alexman's vanilla/PTW formula (CFC 19922). Tavis (Firaxis) says C3C changed only the FP and ranking parts.
6. **Vanilla/PTW science rate caps. RESOLVED (§6):** none. [BIQ-V] and [BIQ-PTW] have `ScienceRateCap=10` for all governments.
7. **Running out of gold. LARGELY RESOLVED (§7, C3X-THREAD code readings):** a building is deleted from a random city with no gold paid; at most one unit is disbanded per turn, the least valuable one, skipping King and flag units (and wrongly not checking `RequireSupport`). Earlier text, kept for the record: REOPENED (§7, third round). The order earlier taken from [C3X-INI] (sell non-gold buildings, disband non-free units, switch to Wealth; AI alternation and research cut) describes the C3X option `aggressively_penalize_bankruptcy` (default off), not stock 1.22. The code shows only that stock pays building and unit upkeep in random order and that its disband search skips King units. Stock rule as sourced: a building is lost (`#MAINTSHORT`) and a unit is disbanded (`#NOSUPPORT`). Still open: whether the lost building is sold for gold, which building or unit is picked, and how many go per turn.
8. **Pollution details.** The per-turn chance per pollution point (1% is a hedged recollection), the yields of a polluted tile (zero per [MANUAL], −1 by the crater analogy) are still unsourced (§8; Disputed 15). *Third round:* the threshold has no tech term. Antal1987's disassembly of `class_City::Calculate_Pollution` uses `General_Settings.Max_City_Size` (12) only. The stock tile search starts at a random one of the 20 radius tiles (C3X-SRC).
9. **Global warming.** The trigger threshold, the number of tiles changed and the frequency are unsourced. Firaxis is reported second-hand to count only city pollution icons and nukes, not ground pollution (§8). The BIQ PollutionEffect for Marsh points at index 11 (Coast). [SCRIPT] `#GLOBALWARMING_RISING_COASTLINES` suggests that is intended.
10. **WLTKD under Despotism. RESOLVED (§10):** no. Firaxis' WLTKD effect list (Magaha) has no tile-penalty item.
11. **Joining past the size caps.** Can a Settler or Worker join a city past its Aqueduct or Hospital cap? Is there a maximum city size in C3C? Not answered by CFC 121112 or 87448. Also open: what happens to surplus food in a city that is capped at 6 or 12.
12. **Minimum city distance. RESOLVED (§11):** no city may be founded next to another ([MANUAL]).
13. **Settler at small size, abandoning and razing. PARTLY RESOLVED (§11):** a non-growing size-2 city that finishes a Settler offers to abandon itself (2005 reports), abandoned cities leave ruins, and razing gives slave Workers in C3C. Still open: the exact slave count per citizen, when the raze option is withheld, and the exact size test.
14. **City center tile. LARGELY RESOLVED (§1):** food is fixed at 2 (Agricultural +1), shields are at least 1 with +1 at size 7 and +1 more at 13 (Industrious +1 at 13 in PTW/C3C), and commerce follows a size table with a capital minimum. All of this is community-tested, with no Firaxis text. Still open: the exact Commercial bonus (Disputed 11).

Still open: whether a Golden Age that starts under Despotism carries over a revolution, and whether the C3C Age of Science really does nothing (CFC 104294).
17. **Web search budget.** In this pass one search was used (city-tile yield). Global warming thresholds and the pollution chance had no further search. The remaining unread sources are listed in `reading-log-02.md`.
